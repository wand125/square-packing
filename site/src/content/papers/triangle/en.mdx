## Abstract

Let $s_\triangle(n)$ be the side of the smallest equilateral triangle that contains $n$ non-overlapping unit squares, at any positions and angles. We prove $s_\triangle(n)$ for $n = 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 11, 16, 22, 29$ and $37$; in particular $s_\triangle(37) = 9 + 2/\sqrt3 \approx 10.15470$. Each upper bound is a packing with exact coordinates, checked exactly (in Lean 4 for $n = 2, 3, 4$ and the series, by two exact checkers for $n = 37$). Erich Friedman's Packing Center, "Squares in Triangles", is cited as the source of the configurations; no originality is claimed for them. Each lower bound is a finite weighted point set: every closed unit square in the triangle captures weight $\ge 1$, and the total weight is $< n$. For $n = 7, \dots, 29$ the certificate is a patch of the unit triangular lattice; at $n = 37$ the axis-parallel rows hold only 36 squares, and a different packing reaches the bound. For $n = 6$ one certificate is not enough, and the proof is a localization tree of six certificates closed by a lemma on three squares. The results are computer-assisted and checked by two independent checkers; all except $n = 6$ are also checked in Lean 4. They have not been peer reviewed.

## 1. The problem and the results

$T_v$ is the equilateral triangle of side $v$ with vertices $(0,0)$, $(v,0)$, $(v/2, v\sqrt3/2)$. $s_\triangle(n)$ is the smallest $v$ such that $n$ unit squares with pairwise disjoint interiors, at any positions and angles, fit in $T_v$.

| 4 | $3 + 2/\sqrt3$ | 4.1547 | three squares on the base, one on top | 3 points of weight 1 (total 3) and Lemma P |

The upper bounds are packings with exact coordinates: proved in Lean 4 for $n = 2, 3, 4$ and the series, given as a packing certificate checked by two exact checkers for $n = 37$, and given by exact coordinates for $n = 6$ (§4). The configurations are those listed in Friedman's table, which is cited as their source only. Friedman's table lists $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ as "Trivial"; the proof for $n = 2$ is included because it is the simplest instance of the method. $n = 5$ is not done (§9).

## 2. The method: weighted point sets

Each lower bound is a finite weighted point set in $T_v$, where $v$ is the claimed value. The checkers prove two things.

1. **Capture.** Every closed unit square $Q \subseteq T_v$, at any position and any angle, contains points of total weight $\ge 1$. A point on the boundary of $Q$ counts. This is checked at the container itself, with margin zero.
2. **Budget.** The total weight is $< n$.

Then $n$ squares do not fit in a triangle of side $L < v$. Suppose they did, with disjoint interiors, and put $\lambda = v/L > 1$. Translate each square $Q_i$ (centre $c_i$) by $(\lambda - 1)(c_i - G)$, where $G$ is the common centroid.

- **Containment.** For $p \in Q_i$, $$ p + (\lambda-1)(c_i - G) - G = \lambda\Big[\tfrac{p - G}{\lambda} + \big(1 - \tfrac1\lambda\big)(c_i - G)\Big], $$ and the bracket is a convex combination of points of $T_L - G$; so the translated square lies in $\lambda(T_L - G) = T_v - G$.
- **Disjointness.** If $u$ is a direction that separates the interiors, then $u\cdot(c_2 - c_1) \ge h_{Q_1}(u) + h_{Q_2}(u) > 0$, and the translation increases this gap by $(\lambda-1)\,u\cdot(c_2 - c_1) > 0$. So the translated closed squares are pairwise disjoint.

Each translated square captures weight $\ge 1$ and each point is counted at most once, so the total would be $\ge n$, which contradicts the budget. With a packing of side $v$, this gives $s_\triangle(n) = v$.

**Lemma P (used for $n = 4$ and the series).** For two points $p, q$ at distance exactly 1, a square whose chord along the line $pq$ crosses two opposite edges and meets the segment $[p, q]$ contains $p$ or $q$. For $n = 4$ this handles the middle square of the base row, which holds both lower points on its two edges.

## 3. n = 2, 3, 4

For $n = 2, 3, 4$ the upper bound is proved in Lean 4 from explicit coordinates in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ (§8).

**$n = 2$, $v = 2 + 2/\sqrt3$.** Two squares side by side on the base; at height 1 the triangle has width $v - 2/\sqrt3 = 2$. The certificate is one point, the centroid $G$, with weight 1. *Lemma:* every closed unit square $Q \subseteq T_v$ contains $G$. By the symmetry of the triangle and of the square it is enough to take $\theta \in [0, \pi/12]$. The admissible centres form a triangle $D_\theta$ whose vertices have closed forms in $\theta$, and $G \in Q$ for every admissible centre if and only if it holds at the three vertices: twelve functions $g(\theta) \ge 0$ on $[0, \pi/12]$. A stand-alone script checks them with exact values in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ at $\theta = 0$, a second-order bound on $[0, 1/100]$ and interval enclosures on 400 sub-intervals of $[1/100, \pi/12]$. Two functions vanish at $\theta = 0$ (the two bottom-corner squares of the extremal packing), with derivatives $(2\sqrt3 - 2)/3$ and $(2 - \sqrt3)/3$, both $> 0$. Two disjoint squares cannot both contain $G$.

**$n = 3$, $v = 3/2 + \sqrt3$.** The packing is the pinwheel listed in Friedman's table (1997); no originality is claimed for it. Its upper bound is the Lean proof; the script `n3_pinwheel.py` is only a floating-point sanity check of the configuration, not part of the proof. Each square has one side on a side of the triangle, placed rotationally symmetrically, and each square touches the next at a single point. The certificate has seven points of weight 1/3, total $7/3 < 3$: the centroid and the six contact points of the pinwheel and of its mirror image (the $D_3$-orbit of $(1 + \sqrt3/3, \sqrt3/2)$). Every closed unit square in $T_v$ contains at least three of them. The tightest poses are squares standing on the base in a bottom corner: at $\theta = 0$ they capture exactly three points, and the worst margin grows like $\theta^2$ and stays about $10^{-4}$ for $\theta \in [3^\circ, 5^\circ]$, so the subdivision goes to depth 30 there.

**$n = 4$, $v = 3 + 2/\sqrt3$.** Three squares side by side on the base and one on top (as in Friedman's table, 1997); at height 1 the width is 3, at height 2 it is $3 - 2/\sqrt3 > 1$. The certificate has three points of weight 1 on the medians at distance $1/\sqrt3$ from the centroid:

$$
\big(1 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3},\ \tfrac13 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\big),\quad \big(2 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3},\ \tfrac13 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}\big),\quad \big(\tfrac32 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3},\ \tfrac13 + \tfrac{5\sqrt3}{6}\big).
$$

They form an equilateral triangle of side exactly 1, and the two lower points lie on the vertical edges shared by the squares of the base row. Every closed unit square in $T_v$ contains one of them. Lemma P is needed: the middle square of the base row holds both lower points on its edges, and which one survives a small perturbation depends on the direction. Without Lemma P the certificate does not verify at depth 14 and did not finish in 30 minutes at depth 40.

## 4. n = 6: a localization tree

**$v = 2 + 4/\sqrt3$.** The packing is a pinwheel of three pairs of side-by-side squares, one pair on each side, with $C_3$ symmetry, as in Friedman's table; its exact coordinates are in `n6_packing.json`. It is held in place by contacts between squares, not only by the walls, so the lower bound needs more than one point certificate. A stage certificate may have *exclusion boxes* (poses it leaves to later stages) and *obstacle points* (squares already placed in their boxes, given by points that every square disjoint from them avoids). If no remaining square is in an exclusion box, all of them capture weight $\ge 1$; the certificate's total is below the number of remaining squares, so some square is in an exclusion box. Labels name positions of the pinwheel; $C_3$ acts as $0\to2\to4$, $1\to3\to5$.

1. **Stage 1.** A $D_3$ certificate with 138 points and total $137013/22900 < 6$, whose exclusions are small boxes around the pinwheel positions. After a symmetry, square $X \in \{0, 1\}$ is within 0.01 of its pinwheel pose.
2. **Stage 2.** With $X$ as an obstacle, a certificate with total $< 5$ forces a partner of $X$ into its box ($\pm 0.012$). Case $X = 1$: 4 points, total 4. Case $X = 0$: 14 points, total $14/3$.
3. **Stage 3.** With two squares in their boxes, a certificate with total $< 4$ forces the third square of a $C_3$ image of the triple $\{0, 1, 3\}$ into its box. Rotating a max-norm box by $120^\circ$ enlarges it by $1/2 + \sqrt3/2$, so the obstacles use $D = 0.0164$, $D_\theta = 0.012$. The three cases have totals $7/2$, 3 and 3.
4. **Leaf.** A lemma on three squares shows that squares 0, 1 and 3 in these boxes need $L \ge v$. With strictly disjoint squares this is a contradiction.

The obstacle points are of three kinds, each proved for every pose of the obstacle square in its box: inner points (an exact inequality), wall witnesses (interval arithmetic plus an analytic step) and, in one case, chain witnesses that use Step 1 of the lemma.

**The triple lemma.** Suppose squares 0, 1 and 3 lie in $T_L$ with pairwise disjoint interiors, tilts $\le \Phi$, corner shifts of squares 0 and 1 $\le \delta$, and the contact parameter $t \in [\tau, 1 - \tau]$, where $t$ is the position of the top-right corner $P$ of square 1 along the inner edge of square 3 (at the pinwheel $t = 2/\sqrt3 - 1 \approx 0.1547$). Then $L \ge v$. In the proof, square 3 becomes a slab: $g_R := d_R(P) - \cos\varphi_3 - \tau|\sin\varphi_3| \ge 0$. With the wall constraints and a separating axis between squares 0 and 1, the weighted sum

$$
S := g_L + g_R + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\, g_{\mathrm{sep}} + \tfrac12\,(g_{b,0} + g_{b,1}) \ \ge\ \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}(v - L)
$$

has weights equal to the KKT multipliers of the reduced problem. Choosing the remaining weights by the signs of $(\varphi_0, \varphi_1)$, interval arithmetic shows

$$
S \le -c\,(|\varphi_0| + |\varphi_1| + |\varphi_3|), \quad c > 0, \tag{M}
$$

for all 2 cases × 8 orthants. Hence $0 \le \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}(v - L) \le S \le -c\|\varphi\|_1$, so $L \ge v$. Without the hypothesis on $t$ the lemma is false: if square 3 slides up by more than 0.155, squares 0 and 1 are free to move right, and the triple alone is not rigid. In the full packing squares 2, 4 and 5 bound the slide; the localization supplies this bound.

## 5. The series n = 7, 11, 16, 22, 29: a triangular lattice patch

For $m \ge 3$ let $v_m = m + 2/\sqrt3$ and $n_m = m(m-1)/2 + 1 = T_{m-1} + 1$.

**Upper bound.** Axis-parallel rows standing on the base. At height $j$ the triangle has width $v_m - 2j/\sqrt3$, so row $j$ holds $\lfloor m - 2j/\sqrt3 \rfloor$ squares. Only the bottom row is tight: at height 1 the width is exactly $m$. The rows packing is proved in Lean 4 for $m = 4, \dots, 8$ (§8).

**Certificate.** Take the points of the unit triangular lattice that form an equilateral triangle of side $m - 2$, that is $T_{m-1}$ points of weight 1, placed so that its centroid is the centroid of the container. The bottom row then lies at height $1/3 + \sqrt3/3$ for every $m$, at $x = 1 + 1/\sqrt3 + i$; these are exactly the shared vertical edges of a tight bottom row of $m$ squares. All pairs at distance 1 are listed for Lemma P. The certificate is invariant under the symmetry group of the triangle, and $m = 3$ gives the $n = 4$ certificate. Its total weight $T_{m-1}$ is smaller than $n_m$, so it proves $s_\triangle(n_m) \ge v_m$.

For $3 \le m \le 8$ the rows hold exactly $n_m$ squares, so $s_\triangle(n_m) = v_m$:

## 6. n = 37: a different packing, and why the rows stop at 36

Let $v = 9 + 2/\sqrt3 \approx 10.15470$.

**Lower bound.** For $m = 9$ the lattice certificate has 36 points of weight 1, total $36 < 37$, and every closed unit square in $T_v$ contains one of them (7668 nodes, maximum depth 25 with limit 40). So no 37 non-overlapping unit squares fit in a smaller equilateral triangle.

**Why the rows give only 36.** In $T_v$ axis-parallel rows standing on the base hold $9 + 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 36$ squares; the eighth row does not fit, because at height 7 the width is $9 - 14/\sqrt3 \approx 0.917 < 1$. In general the lattice triangle has about $m^2/2$ points, while the number of squares that fit in the triangle of side $v_m$ grows only like its area, about $(\sqrt3/4)m^2 \approx 0.433m^2$. So for large $m$ the certificate gives a valid but weak lower bound; it is not a route to a statement for all $m$.

**Upper bound.** A packing with exact coordinates in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$. A tight bottom row of nine axis-parallel squares stands on the base, at $x = 1/\sqrt3 + i$ ($i = 0, \dots, 8$). Above height 1 lies an equilateral triangle of side 9. It holds 28 squares in the shape of Friedman's $n = 28$ packing (side $3 + 10/\sqrt3 < 9$): axis-parallel rows of 7, 6, 3 and 2 squares, each pushed against the left side, and two blocks of 3 + 2 squares, one aligned with each slanted side. Since $3 + 10/\sqrt3 < 9$ there is room to spare. The packing is checked by two independently written exact checkers (§8), both of which reject five negative controls. So

$$
s_\triangle(37) = 9 + \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3}.
$$

We do not claim this construction as new: Friedman's table lists a packing of 37 squares by Gianluca Costantino (May 2026), and the upper part uses the shape of Friedman's $n = 28$ packing (1997).

**Depth.** A first run of the lower-bound check with depth limit 22 stopped with 35 uncertified boxes along the left side, at angles $\theta \approx 10.7^\circ$–$16^\circ$, where the square has a corner pointing at the side. This was inconclusive, not a counterexample. A local float search finds the tightest pose near centre $(1.6496, 1.4430)$, $\theta = 14.44^\circ$, with margin 0.0025. With depth limit 40 the checker verifies the lemma.

**Friedman's table.** At the time of writing (2026-10-01), Friedman's page lists $n = 37$ with side 10.15299 (Gianluca Costantino, May 2026). This is smaller than the lower bound above, so we take the listed value to be a typo. The picture on that page appears to show a bottom row of nine axis-parallel squares standing on the base; nine such squares need width 9 at height 1, that is, side at least $9 + 2/\sqrt3$. We do not have the coordinates of that packing; our own packing has side exactly $9 + 2/\sqrt3$.

## 7. Conclusion

For $n = 2, 3, 4$ and $n = 7, 11, 16, 22, 29$, a single weighted point set with total weight $< n$ captures weight $\ge 1$ in every closed unit square of $T_v$; with the centre-scaling argument of §2 and the packings this gives

$$
s_\triangle(2) = 2 + \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3},\quad s_\triangle(3) = \tfrac32 + \sqrt3,\quad s_\triangle(4) = 3 + \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3},\quad s_\triangle(T_{m-1} + 1) = m + \tfrac{2}{\sqrt3}\ \ (m = 4, \dots, 8).
$$

For $n = 6$ the localization tree of §4 leaves every branch in a leaf closed by the triple lemma, so $s_\triangle(6) = 2 + 4/\sqrt3$. For $n = 37$ the lattice certificate gives the lower bound and the packing of §6 the upper bound, so $s_\triangle(37) = 9 + 2/\sqrt3$.

|---|---|---|
| checker 1 | subdivides pose space (centre box × angle bin, $u = \tan(\theta/2)$); for fixed $u$ the admissible centres in a box form a convex polygon whose vertices are rational functions of $u$, and capture is checked at the vertices with exact Bernstein bounds over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$; uses $D_3$ symmetry and Lemma P; standard library only | all certificates, including the six of $n = 6$ |
| checker 2 | written from the specification, the proof notes and the certificate format, without opening checker 1's code; an exact angular sweep of a line arrangement over all $u \in [0, 1]$: critical angles from the real roots of concurrency and parallelism determinants, one rational sample in every gap, closure by upper semicontinuity; no box subdivision, no Bernstein bounds, no symmetry reduction, no pair lemma | $n = 2, 3, 4$ and the series up to $n = 37$ |
| checker 2, $n = 6$ | re-checks every part of the $n = 6$ proof by other methods: a cell grid for the certificates (with an exact angular sweep for one window of stage 1), interval branch and bound for the obstacle points, a sympy rederivation and interval check of (M), checks of the lemma's hypotheses and of the links between stages | $n = 6$ |
| `check_sat.py`, `check_clip.py` (packing) | two independently written exact checkers in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$: unit squares, every corner in the closed triangle and a separating edge normal for every pair (sympy); sides 1 and area 1, area 1 after clipping by the triangle and area 0 for every pairwise intersection (own number class); the file is also in the shared format `packing-certificate/1` and passes the repository's common checker `tools/packing_check/check_packing.py` | the $n = 37$ packing (upper bound); five negative controls are rejected |

checker 2 results for the series: critical angles 1,231 ($n = 7$), 2,311, 3,853, 5,959, 8,767 and 12,157 ($n = 37$), minimum weight 1 in every case. Negative controls (side $L + 1/100$, a point shifted, a point dropped, a weight lowered, an exclusion or the obstacle points removed) fail as expected.

**Lean 4.** Kernel-checked proofs of $s_\triangle(n)$ for $n = 2, 3, 4, 7, 11, 16, 22, 29$ and of the bound for $n = 37$ ($\forall s,\ \mathrm{PacksTri}\ 37\ s \to 9 + 2/\sqrt3 \le s$), with only the standard axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`; no `sorry`, no `native_decide`). The lower bounds use a third checker, proved sound in Lean once: each leaf of a box tree carries a Farkas–Bernstein certificate, a polynomial identity with nonnegative multipliers over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ that the kernel checks coefficient by coefficient. It covers all angles directly and uses neither the $D_3$ symmetry nor Lemma P. The upper bounds for $n = 2, 3, 4$ and the series are explicit packings with centres, cosines and sines in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$, checked by exact tests for containment and for disjointness (a separating edge normal) with soundness proofs; for the series one Boolean test checks the axis-parallel rows. The Python generator and both Python checkers are not trusted; the trusted base is the Lean kernel, Lean core, Mathlib and the definitions in the statements. Leaves: 3, 86, 240 for $n = 2, 3, 4$; 1,013, 1,863, 3,753, 6,884, 8,661 and 9,776 for $n = 7, \dots, 37$. $n = 6$ is not formalised in Lean.

## 9. Status and limitations

- Computer-assisted proofs. They have not been peer reviewed.
- $n = 6$ is checked by two independent systems but is not formalised in Lean.
- For $n = 3, 4, 6, 7, 11, 16, 22, 29$ we are not aware of a previous proof of optimality. We checked Friedman's page (as of 2026-09-30), which gives the packings but no proofs or lower bounds, an arXiv search of abstracts, and general web searches, and did not find a lower bound for this container. If you know of one, please open an issue. For $n = 2$ no novelty is claimed.
- For $n = 37$ the upper bound is checked by two exact checkers; the lower bound is also proved in Lean 4, the upper bound is not.
- $n = 5$ is not done. Its best known side is $2 + 4/\sqrt3$, the same as $s_\triangle(6)$, so $s_\triangle(5) \le s_\triangle(6) = 2 + 4/\sqrt3$; the lower bound is work in progress.
