## Abstract

Let $s(n)$ be the smallest side of a regular hexagon that contains $n$ non-overlapping unit squares, at arbitrary positions and orientations. We prove that $s(3) = (1+\sqrt3)/2$. The upper bound is given by our packing certificate for the pinwheel, verified in exact arithmetic by two checkers. This packing is listed in Erich Friedman's Packing Center, and we claim no originality for it. Because the pinwheel is not rigid to first order, a single set of weighted points cannot establish the lower bound. Our proof combines a stage-1 point certificate with 742 excused boxes, a local lemma near the pinwheel configuration, and a stage-2 branch-and-bound search over each excused box. Each stage was verified by two independent implementations. This proof has not been peer reviewed and is not formalised in Lean.

## 1. The pinwheel (upper bound)

Let $H_v$ be the regular hexagon of side $v$ centred at $0$ with a horizontal bottom edge. Let $h = v\sqrt3/2$ be its apothem, let $V = (-v/2, -h)$ be its lower-left vertex, and let $R$ be rotation by $2\pi/3$. The pinwheel is

$$
B = [-\tfrac v2, -\tfrac v2 + 1] \times [-h, -h+1], \qquad RB, \qquad R^2B.
$$

$B$ lies on the bottom side with its lower-left corner at $V$, and the top-right corner of $R^iB$ lies on the top edge of $R^{i+1}B$.

**Upper-bound certificate.** The file [n3/packing.json](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/hexagon/n3/packing.json) provides these three squares with exact coordinates in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$. The two checkers in [tools/packing_check/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/tools/packing_check) verify in exact arithmetic that each square lies in the closed hexagon $H_v$ and that no two interiors intersect: one checker tests vertices and separating axes, while the other computes exact polygon clipping areas. This packing is listed in Friedman's Packing Center (due to Morandi); we claim no originality for it.

## 2. Reduction to closed squares

Suppose three unit squares with disjoint interiors fit into $H_L$ with $L < v$. Scale their centres away from the origin by the factor $k = v/L$. If $Q \subseteq H_L$ has centre $c$, every point $q + (k-1)c$ of $Q + (k-1)c$, with $q \in Q$, can be written as $k\,(q/k + (1-1/k)c)$. The term in parentheses is a convex combination of points in $H_L$, so $Q + (k-1)c \subseteq kH_L = H_v$. The translated squares are pairwise disjoint even as *closed* squares: if a direction $u$ separates the interiors of two of them, then $u\cdot(c_2 - c_1) \ge h(u) + h'(u) \ge 1$, where $h, h'$ are the support functions of the two squares translated to the origin, and the translation increases $u\cdot(c_2 - c_1)$ by $(k-1)\,u\cdot(c_2 - c_1) > 0$. It therefore suffices to show that $H_v$ contains no three pairwise disjoint closed unit squares.

## 3. Stage 1: one square lies in the flex tube

The certificate consists of 630 weighted points with total weight

$$
\sum w = \frac{749319}{250000} < 3
$$

along with 742 *excused boxes* in pose space (centre, $u = \tan(\theta/2)$) over the reduced range $u \in [0, 1/7]$. Under the action of $D_6$, these boxes cover a tubular neighbourhood of radius 0.03 around the *flex curve*: the square $B$ rotated about $V$ by angles in $[-0.03, 0.52]$, together with its images under $D_6$.

**Claim.** Every closed unit square in $H_v$ whose reduced pose is not excused captures weight $\ge 1$.

Three disjoint squares capture less than 3 in total, so at least one square captures weight strictly less than 1. Since capture is $D_6$-invariant, after applying a symmetry square 1 has its pose in some excused box $E_k$. A control calculation shows that these boxes are necessary: an admissible pose inside an excused box captures only $495031/500000 < 1$.

## 4. The local lemma near the pinwheel

In frame $i$ (rotated by $R^{-i}$), square $i$ is $V + p_i + R(\varphi_i)[0,1]^2$, with corner displacement $p_i$ and tilt $\varphi_i$. The pinwheel configuration corresponds to $p_i = 0,\ \varphi_i = 0$.

**Lemma.** If $|\varphi_i| \le 0.08$ and $|p_i| \le 0.05$ for $i = 0, 1, 2$, the three closed squares cannot simultaneously lie in $H_v$ and be pairwise disjoint. The same holds for every image of the pinwheel under $D_6$.

Containment puts each $p_i$ in the $120^\circ$ wedge at the vertex. The contacts satisfy the exact identity (checked in sympy)

$$
k_i = F(\varphi_i, \varphi_{i+1}) + \big(R(\varphi_{i+1})\,n\big)\cdot p_i - \big(R(\varphi_{i+1})\,e_y\big)\cdot p_{i+1},
$$

and, while the contact corners stay inside the other edge lines (this holds throughout the neighbourhood of the lemma: at the pinwheel such a corner is at distances 0.366, 0.634 and 1 from the other three edge lines, and these change by at most 0.316),

$$
\text{(i)}\ \ k_i \le F_i + \Phi\,(|p_i| + |p_{i+1}|), \qquad \text{(ii)}\ \ \textstyle\sum k \le \sum F - \big(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} - 2\Phi\big) \sum |p|.
$$

If the tilts are equal (along the flex), $\sum F \le 0$; otherwise some $F_j$ is negative to first order. In either case, some contact satisfies $k_i \le 0$, which contradicts disjointness. The radius conditions are verified in exact rational arithmetic.

## 5. Stage 2: branch and bound for each excused box

For each excused box $E_k$, a branch-and-bound search proves that no three pairwise disjoint closed unit squares in $H_v$ can have the pose of square 1 in $E_k$, where squares 2 and 3 range over all poses. A node is a triple of pose boxes. It is discarded when

- **outside:** some square extends outside $H_v$ for every pose in its box;
- **overlap:** two squares intersect for every pair of poses;
- **lemma:** inequality (i) or (ii), applied only where its hypothesis holds, implies that some contact satisfies $k_i \le 0$, or all three boxes lie in the neighbourhood of the lemma.

Otherwise, the largest box is bisected. All operations use interval arithmetic rounded outward; $\sqrt3$ and $\pi$ are enclosed by adjacent doubles, and the excused boxes are read from the exact rationals in the certificate. All 742 boxes are successfully proved, leaving 0 remaining leaves.

## 6. Conclusion

By §3, any three pairwise disjoint closed unit squares in $H_v$ must have at least one square in some excused box $E_k$. By §5, this is impossible for every $k$. Hence $H_v$ contains no such triple, and by §2 we have $s(3) \ge v$. Together with the pinwheel packing (§1),

$$
s(3) = v = \frac{1+\sqrt3}{2}. \qquad \blacksquare
$$

## 7. Verification

The second implementation of each stage was written independently, without reading the code of the first.

| Stage | Implementation 1 | Implementation 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | exact $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ subdivision with Bernstein bounds; 5,768 nodes | exact angular line-arrangement sweep, from the specification only; 58,894 boxes |
| 2 | Python + numba interval branch and bound; 742/742 proved, $5.75\cdot10^9$ nodes | Rust interval branch and bound, from the specification and the lemma only; 742/742 proved, $4.37\cdot10^9$ nodes |

**Controls.** Without the local lemma, the box containing the pinwheel square is not proved, and the remaining leaves lie close to the pinwheel. At side $1.01\,v$, the scaled pinwheel is a genuine packing, and neither implementation's outside or overlap test discards it. A fuzz test of the pruning tests in implementation 1 found 0 violations across 20,000 random nodes.

## 8. Status and limitations

- A computer-assisted proof. It has not been peer reviewed and has not been formalised in Lean.
- We are not aware of any previous proof that $s(3) = (1+\sqrt3)/2$; if you know of one, please open an issue.
- Other values of $n$ are not treated here.
