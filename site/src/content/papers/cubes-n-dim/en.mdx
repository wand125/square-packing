## Abstract

Let $s_n(k)$ be the side of the smallest $n$-cube that contains $k$ unit $n$-cubes with pairwise disjoint interiors, at any positions and orientations. We prove $s_4(2) = 2$: two unit 4-cubes do not fit in a 4-cube of side less than 2. The upper bound is the trivial packing. The lower bound follows from a centre lemma: every unit 4-cube inside $[0, 2]^4$ contains the centre of $[0, 2]^4$. Near the coordinate axes the lemma is proved by hand, using Lemma F in dimension 3; away from them it is checked by interval branch and bound with outward rounding, by two independent checkers. The result is computer-assisted and has not been peer reviewed.

Version 2 (§7) proves $s_n(2) = 2$ for $n = 3, 4, 5$: near the axes by the argument of §3, elsewhere by a spectral lower bound of $1 - F$ with short exact rational certificates, checked by two systems in seconds. Its $n = 5$ proof and first $n = 5$ certificate were produced with Codex (OpenAI). It also proves $s_6(2) = 2$, where the spectral bound alone fails, by a split into a cap, a strip and a near-axis region; the $n = 6$ proof and certificates were also produced with Codex (OpenAI).

## Notation

For $R \in O(n)$ write $a_{ik} = |R_{ik}|$, $w_i = \sum_j |R_{ij}|$ (the $\ell^1$ norm of row $i$, i.e. the width of the cube $R[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^n$ in direction $e_i$), and

$$
F_k(R) = \sum_i a_{ik}\,(2 - w_i).
$$

$F_k$ depends only on $|R|$. It is therefore invariant under permutations and sign changes of rows and of columns (with the column index $k$ moved accordingly), and under $R \mapsto -R$.

**Theorem.** Two unit 4-cubes with disjoint interiors do not fit in a 4-cube of side $a < 2$. Hence $s_4(2) = 2$.

The upper bound $s_4(2) \le 2$ is the trivial packing (two cubes side by side in $[0, 2]^4$).

## 1. Reduction to Lemma F₄

**Lemma F₄.** $F_k(R) \le 1$ for every $R \in O(4)$ and every column $k$.

**Lemma F₄ implies the theorem.** The argument is the one for $n = 3$ in [cubes-in-cube/lemma_f/PROOF.md](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/cubes-in-cube/lemma_f/PROOF.md) and works in any dimension $n$.

- Let $a < 2$ and let $C = c + R[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^n$ lie in $K_a = [-a/2, a/2]^n$. We show that the centre $0$ of $K_a$ is in the interior of $C$.
- Suppose not. Since $C = \{x : \lVert R^{\mathsf T}(x - c)\rVert_\infty \le \tfrac12\}$, some column $u_k$ of $R$ has $|u_k \cdot c| \ge \tfrac12$.
- Containment means $|c_i| + w_i/2 \le a/2$ for every $i$, so $a - w_i \ge 0$.
- Hence
  $$
  |u_k \cdot c| \le \sum_i a_{ik}|c_i| \le \sum_i a_{ik}\,\frac{a - w_i}{2} < \sum_i a_{ik}\,\frac{2 - w_i}{2} = \frac{F_k}{2}.
  $$
  The inequality is strict because $a < 2$ and $\sum_i a_{ik} \ge 1$.
- With $F_k \le 1$ this gives $|u_k \cdot c| < \tfrac12$, a contradiction.

So every unit cube in $K_a$ contains the centre in its interior, and two cubes with disjoint interiors cannot both lie in $K_a$. ∎

(Conversely, the statement "every unit $n$-cube in $[0, 2]^n$ contains the centre" is equivalent to $F_k \le 1$ for all $R \in O(n)$ and all $k$; only the direction above is needed.)

By the symmetries, it suffices to prove $F_1(R) \le 1$, and we may assume that the largest $|R_{e1}|$ is $R_{11} \ge 0$. Write $u$ for column 1, $c = R_{11} = \max_e |u_e| = \cos\theta$ with $0 \le \theta < \pi/2$, $x = (R_{21}, R_{31}, R_{41})$ (so $\lVert x\rVert_2 = \sin\theta$), and $M$ for the lower-right $3 \times 3$ block of $R$. The proof splits at $c^2 = 2/3$:

- **near region** $c^2 \ge 2/3$: a hand proof (§3), which uses Lemma F in dimension 3;
- **far region** $c^2 \le 2/3$: two independent computer checks (§4).

The figure shows $F_1$ against $c^2$ for a rotation of $\mathbb R^4$ that you set with six sliders, together with random rotations. $F_1 = 1$ at $R = I$; the rotation by $\arccos\sqrt{2/3}$ in the plane $(1, 2)$ puts $c^2$ on $2/3$.

## 2. Lemma F in dimension 3

**Lemma F₃.** $F_k(Q) \le 1$ for every $Q \in O(3)$ and every column $k$.

This is Lemma F of [cubes-in-cube/lemma_f/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-in-cube/lemma_f) (a hand proof near the axes and two independent computer checks elsewhere; see also [the paper on unit cubes in a cube](paper-cubes-in-cube.html), §2). It is stated there for $SO(3)$; since $F$ depends only on $|Q|$, and $-Q \in SO(3)$ when $Q \in O(3)$ has determinant $-1$, it holds on $O(3)$.

## 3. Near region: if c² ≥ 2/3 then F₁ ≤ 1

The steps (a)–(c) are those of the $n = 3$ proof ([lemma_f/PROOF.md](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/cubes-in-cube/lemma_f/PROOF.md) §1) and use only orthogonality, so they hold in any dimension; we write them for $n = 4$.

**(a)** Let $v_i = w_i - a_{i1}$ (the $\ell^1$ norm of row $i$ without its first entry). Then $\sum_i a_{i1} w_i = \sum_i a_{i1}^2 + \sum_i a_{i1} v_i = 1 + \sum_i a_{i1} v_i$, so

$$
1 - F_1 = 2 - 2\lVert u\rVert_1 + \sum_i a_{i1} v_i = 2(1 - c) - 2\lVert x\rVert_1 + c\,v_1 + \sum_{i=2}^{4} |x_i|\,\lVert M_i\rVert_1,
$$

where $M_i$ is row $i$ of $M$ (indexed by the rows 2, 3, 4 of $R$).

**(b)** Columns 1 and $j \ge 2$ are orthogonal: $c R_{1j} + (M^{\mathsf T}x)_j = 0$. Hence $c\,v_1 = \lVert M^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1$.

**(c)** If $\theta = 0$, then $x = 0$, row 1 is $e_1$ (so $v_1 = 0$), and $1 - F_1 = 0$. Otherwise, rows 2–4 of $R$ are orthonormal, so $M M^{\mathsf T} = I - x x^{\mathsf T}$. The polar decomposition is $M = PQ$ with

$$
P = (I - x x^{\mathsf T})^{1/2} = I - (1 - c)\,\hat x \hat x^{\mathsf T}, \qquad \hat x = x/\sin\theta,
$$

which is invertible (eigenvalues 1, 1 and $c > 0$), so $Q = P^{-1}M \in O(3)$. Since $Px = c\,x$, $M^{\mathsf T}x = Q^{\mathsf T}Px = c\,Q^{\mathsf T}x$. Row $i$ of $M$ is $M_i = Q_i - (1 - c)\,\hat x_i\,(\hat x^{\mathsf T}Q)$, so $\lVert M_i\rVert_1 \ge \lVert Q_i\rVert_1 - (1 - c)|\hat x_i|\,\lVert Q^{\mathsf T}\hat x\rVert_1$, and with $\sum_i |x_i||\hat x_i| = \lVert x\rVert_2$,

$$
\sum_i |x_i|\,\lVert M_i\rVert_1 \ge \sum_i |x_i|\,\lVert Q_i\rVert_1 - (1 - c)\,\lVert Q^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1.
$$

**(d)** Put $y = Q^{\mathsf T}x$ and $K(Q, x) = \lVert y\rVert_1 + \sum_i |x_i|\,\lVert Q_i\rVert_1 - 2\lVert x\rVert_1$. Combining (a)–(c), with $c\lVert y\rVert_1 - (1 - c)\lVert y\rVert_1 = \lVert y\rVert_1 - 2(1 - c)\lVert y\rVert_1$,

$$
1 - F_1 \ge K(Q, x) + 2(1 - c)(1 - \lVert y\rVert_1).
$$

**(e)** $\lVert y\rVert_1 \le \sqrt3\,\lVert y\rVert_2 = \sqrt3\,\lVert x\rVert_2 = \sqrt3\sin\theta \le 1$, because $\sin^2\theta = 1 - c^2 \le 1/3$. So the second term is $\ge 0$.

**(f) Lemma K: $K(Q, x) \ge 0$.** Write $x = Qy$, so $|x_i| \le \sum_j |Q_{ij}|\,|y_j|$. For $Q \in O(3)$ every row has $w_i(Q) = \lVert Q_i\rVert_1 \le \sqrt3 < 2$, so the factor $w_i(Q) - 2$ is negative and

$$
K = \lVert y\rVert_1 + \sum_i |x_i|\,(w_i(Q) - 2) \ge \sum_j |y_j|\Bigl(1 - \sum_i |Q_{ij}|\,(2 - w_i(Q))\Bigr) = \sum_j |y_j|\,(1 - F_j(Q)) \ge 0
$$

by Lemma F₃. Hence $F_1 \le 1$ in the near region. ∎

(The same argument proves: if Lemma F holds in dimension $m \le 4$, then in dimension $m + 1$ every column with $\max_e R_{e1}^2 \ge 1 - 1/m$ satisfies $F_1 \le 1$. The bound $w_i \le \sqrt m \le 2$ used in (f) fails for $m \ge 5$.)

**Lean.** Steps (a)–(f) are formalized in [lean/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-n-dim/lean) for any dimension $m$, with Lemma F in dimension $m$ as a hypothesis. `near_identity` is (a)–(d), `polarQ_orth` is the orthogonality of $Q$ in (c), `l1_QTx_le_one` is (e), and `lemmaK` and `rowL1_le_two` are (f). The conclusion of this section is `near_region` with $m = 3$. See [lean/README.md](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/cubes-n-dim/lean/README.md).

## 4. Far region: if c² ≤ 2/3 then F₁ < 1 (computer-assisted)

**Claim.** For every $R \in O(4)$ with $\max_e R_{e1}^2 \le 2/3$, $F_1(R) < 1$.

It suffices to check $SO(4)$: if $\det R = -1$, negating column 4 gives a matrix in $SO(4)$ with the same $|R|$. The claim is checked by two independent branch-and-bound programs in outward-rounded interval arithmetic. The near and far regions overlap on $c^2 = 2/3$, so their union is all of $O(4)$.

| | first system (`checker/`) | second system (`checker2/`) |

| bound on $F$ | mean-value form with interval forward-mode AD, also $2\lVert u\rVert_1 - 1 - \sum a_e \lVert m_e\rVert_1$ and $G(u)$ | mean-value form with interval AD, $F$'s own upper bound only |

## 5. Notes

- The equality set of $F_1 = 1$ is not isolated: $R = \operatorname{diag}(\pm1, S)$ with any $S \in O(n - 1)$ gives $F_1 = 1$. Near it, $1 - F_1 \gtrsim \theta^2$, where $\theta$ is the angle between column 1 and the nearest axis. This is why the near region is handled by hand and the computer checks only need a strict inequality away from it.
- Numerically, the largest value of $F_1$ in the far region is about 0.845, attained near the boundary $c^2 = 2/3$.

## 7. Version 2: n = 3, 4, 5, 6

Version 2 ([spectral/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-n-dim/spectral)) gives a second proof of $s_4(2) = 2$ and extends it to $s_3(2) = 2$, $s_5(2) = 2$ and, at the end of this section, $s_6(2) = 2$. For $n = 3, 4, 5$ it proves Lemma F in dimensions 3, 4 and 5 by induction on the dimension, each step using only Lemma F in the dimension below. The induction starts from Lemma F₂, which has a one-line proof: up to signs a 2-by-2 orthogonal matrix has $|U| = \begin{pmatrix} s & t \\ t & s \end{pmatrix}$ with $s^2 + t^2 = 1$, so with $w = s + t$, $F_j = w(2 - w) = 1 - (1 - w)^2 \le 1$. For a column with squared entries $p_i$:

- **Near an axis**, $\max_i p_i \ge 1 - 1/(n - 1)$: the argument of §3, with Lemma F in dimension $n - 1$. It needs rows of $\ell^1$ norm at most 2 in dimension $n - 1$, which holds for $n \le 5$. The thresholds are $1/2$, $2/3$ and $3/4$ for $n = 3, 4, 5$.
- **Elsewhere.** Change row signs so that the column is $x \ge 0$ and write $U = [x\ C]$. For each column $y$ of $C$, the coordinates of $z = \operatorname{diag}(x)\,y$ sum to zero, so $\lVert z\rVert_1 \ge \sqrt2\,\lVert z\rVert_2$. With the triangle inequality for the nuclear norm this gives

$$
1 - F_j \ge q(p) = \sqrt2\,\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\operatorname{diag}(p) - p p^\top} - 2\sum_i \sqrt{p_i} + 2 .
$$

A certificate proves $q(p) > 0$ on the ordered probability simplex with $p_1 \le 1 - 1/(n - 1)$. It is a branch and bound on simplices, split at exact rational midpoints. Inside a simplex, the concavity of the trace square root bounds the trace term from below by its values at the vertices, and the square-root terms are bounded from above: in each coordinate $\sqrt t$ lies above its chord between the smallest and largest vertex values $l, u$, by at most $(\sqrt u - \sqrt l)^2 / (4(\sqrt u + \sqrt l))$, so $\sqrt{p_i}$ exceeds the convex combination of its vertex values by at most this amount. At each vertex a symmetric rational witness matrix with Weyl's inequality bounds the trace term. Every acceptance is an exact rational inequality.

$q$ is negative at some points near a coordinate vector (for example $q \approx -0.025$ at $p = (0.96, 0.01, 0.01, 0.01, 0.01)$), which is why the near-axis step is needed. The spectral bound alone fails for $n = 6$: there $q$ is already negative at the uniform $p$ ($\approx -0.0122$). Dimension 6 is proved with a different split, below.

## Status and limitations

- A computer-assisted proof. It has not been peer reviewed.
- Lean: the hand part of the near region (§3) is formalized in Lean 4 + Mathlib for any dimension: the polar-decomposition inequality, the orthogonality of the explicit polar factor, and Lemma K. The main theorem `near_region` takes Lemma F in dimension $m$ as an explicit hypothesis and gives $F_1 \le 1$ for $m \le 4$ when $c^2 \ge 1 - 1/m$. It has no `sorry`, and its only axioms are Lean's standard three. The reduction of §1, Lemma F in dimension 3 and the far region of §4 are not formalized.
- The hand parts (§1–3: the reduction, the polar-decomposition steps in dimension 4, and Lemma K) were checked line by line by an independent internal reviewer. That reviewer also checked the reductions of the first system (the 384 signed permutations, the Voronoi cell, the passage from $O(3)$ to $SO(3)$, and the Householder formula) and the specification of the second system, including its two corrections made during implementation.
- Version 2 (§7) is also computer-assisted, with exact rational certificates checked by two systems. Its $n = 5$ proof and first $n = 5$ certificate were produced with Codex (OpenAI); we reviewed each step of that proof. Its near-axis step for $n = 3, 4, 5$ is §3, formalized in Lean as above. The $n = 6$ proof and certificates were also produced with Codex (OpenAI), and we checked each step of the written proof.
- No external review yet. Corrections are welcome as issues.
