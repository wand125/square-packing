## Abstract

Let $s_4(k)$ be the side of the smallest 4-cube that contains $k$ unit 4-cubes with pairwise disjoint interiors, at any positions and orientations. We give packings with exact certificates (written in a quadratic field for $k \le 29$, in rational numbers for $k = 30$) that show
$$
\begin{gathered} s_4(17) \le 2 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}, \\ s_4(k) \le 2 + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2} \quad (18 \le k \le 25), \\ s_4(k) \le 2 + \tfrac{2\sqrt2}{3} \quad (26 \le k \le 29), \\ s_4(30) \le 2.9876881. \end{gathered}
$$
For $k = 27$, $28$ and $29$ the side $2 + 2\sqrt2/3 \approx 2.9428090416$ is below the published values $2.942809214$, $2.942826011$ and $2.942830937$, by $1.7\cdot10^{-7}$, $1.7\cdot10^{-5}$ and $2.2\cdot10^{-5}$. For $k = 26$ it is the closed form the source conjectured for its published value $2.942809042$; for $k = 17$ to $25$ it agrees with the published value to the printed digits, now with an exact packing. Every packing is certified: for $k \le 29$ it is a file of exact numbers in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ or $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, for $k = 30$ a file of rationals, and two checkers written independently decide containment and pairwise separation with exact arithmetic. The packing for $k = 25$ is the product of two packings of 5 unit squares in the square of side $2 + 1/\sqrt2$, and its validity is also checked by hand (§4). The packings for $k = 18$ to $24$ are the one for $k = 25$ with cubes removed, and those for $k = 26$ to $28$ the one for $k = 29$ with cubes removed. For $k = 30$ a rational certificate gives the side $2.9876881$, below the published $2.989568410$ by $0.0018804$ (§6). We prove no lower bounds: whether these sides are optimal is open. The certificates are checked by computer and have not been peer reviewed.

## Notation

- A unit 4-cube is $Q = c + R\,[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^4$ with centre $c \in \mathbb R^4$ and frame $R$, an orthogonal $4 \times 4$ matrix whose columns are the cube axes. The container is $[0, s]^4$.
- Let $h_r = \tfrac12 \sum_m |R_{rm}|$ be half the $\ell^1$ norm of row $r$ of $R$. Then $Q \subseteq [0, s]^4$ if and only if $h_r \le c_r \le s - h_r$ for $r = 1, \dots, 4$.
- A cube is *turned $45°$ in the plane $(x_p, x_q)$* when two of its axes are $(e_p \pm e_q)/\sqrt2$ and the other two are the remaining standard basis vectors. An *axis-parallel* cube has for $R$ a signed permutation matrix.
- *Published* values are those reported by the sources ([Sources](#sources)), as printed there.

For $k = 25$ (and so for $18 \le k \le 24$) the hand proof of §4 also applies. For $k = 30$ the certificate of §6 is accepted by two checkers. $\blacksquare$

The comparisons with published values are exact. For any published value $p$, the inequality $2 + 2\sqrt2/3 < p$ is equivalent to $\sqrt2 < \tfrac32(p - 2)$, which holds for $p = 2.942809214$, $2.942826011$, and $2.942830937$ upon squaring both sides, since the right-hand side is positive. The differences in the table are rounded.

## 2. Certificates and checkers

A certificate (format `alg-v1`) lists $k$ centres and $k$ frames. Each number is stored as a pair of rationals $(a, b)$, representing $a + b\sqrt d$, with $d = 3$ for $k = 17$ and $d = 2$ for $k = 18$ to $29$. The container side is stored in the same format: $(2, \tfrac13)$, $(2, \tfrac12)$, or $(2, \tfrac23)$. A certificate is accepted if and only if, under exact arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt d)$:

1. every frame is orthogonal: $R_i^{\mathsf T} R_i = I$;
2. every cube is contained in the container: $h_r \le c_r \le s - h_r$ for each coordinate;
3. every pair $i < j$ admits a separating axis $a$ among the generalised cross products of $t$ columns of $R_i$ and $3 - t$ columns of $R_j$, for $t = 0, \dots, 3$ (56 candidates):
$$
2\,|a\cdot(c_i - c_j)| \ \ge\ \sum_m |a\cdot R_i e_m| + \sum_m |a\cdot R_j e_m|.
$$

Here the generalised cross product of three vectors $v_1, v_2, v_3 \in \mathbb R^4$ is the vector whose $m$-th entry is $(-1)^m$ times the determinant of the $3 \times 3$ matrix obtained from the rows $v_1, v_2, v_3$ by deleting column $m$ ($m = 0, \dots, 3$); it is orthogonal to $v_1, v_2, v_3$ and is the zero vector exactly when they are linearly dependent. A candidate that is the zero vector is skipped: the certificate must separate every pair along a non-zero candidate. Condition 3 implies that the projections of the two cubes onto the line $\mathbb R a$ overlap in at most one point, ensuring that their interiors are disjoint by the separating-axis theorem for convex polytopes. The sign of $a + b\sqrt d$ is determined exactly by comparing $a^2$ with $d\,b^2$; no floating-point arithmetic enters any decision.

The files named in this paper (`SPEC-ALG.md`, the specification of the certificate and of its canonical form; `NESTING.md`; the checkers) are all in the Release `cubes-n-dim-n4-certs-v3` (§7). The first checker is `check_alg.py`. The second, located in `check2/`, was implemented independently from the specification `SPEC-ALG.md` without inspecting the first. It includes tests that mutate each certificate in several ways to require rejection, together with small hand-crafted cases. Both checkers accept all 13 certificates; each run requires about one second.

The certificates are in a canonical form (`SPEC-ALG.md`): numbers in lowest terms; each frame replaced by the lexicographically largest of its 384 equivalents under the symmetries of the cube; with the frames and the side fixed, the centres of cubes that can move pushed by lexicographic linear programmes, whose optimal vertices are read back exactly; cubes sorted by centre. So a packing has one file and one SHA-256. The tables below are read from these files, and the numbering of the cubes is their order there.

**What holds the side.** The figures below show, for each side, the cubes and walls that hold it: with the frames and the side fixed, the linear programme over the centres that minimises the side, and the contacts and walls with positive multipliers in its dual. For $k = 17$ and $25$ such a chain alone gives the formula of the side, so it is a proof that the side cannot shrink with those frames. For $k = 29$ and $30$ it shows only the active contacts at the optimum with the frames fixed; it is not a proof of local optimality.

## 3. k = 17: side 2 + 1/√3

Let $s = 2 + 1/\sqrt3 = 2 + \sqrt3/3 \approx 2.5773503$. The packing has 16 axis-parallel cubes and one turned cube, number 8, with centre $(1 + \sqrt3/6)(1, 1, 1, 1)$ and frame
$$
R_8 = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & -1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
The rows of $R_8$ are orthogonal and of length 1, so $R_8$ is orthogonal. Each row has three entries $\pm1/\sqrt3$, so $h_r = \tfrac12\cdot 3/\sqrt3 = \sqrt3/2$ for every $r$, and cube 8 occupies $[1 - \sqrt3/3,\ 1 + 2\sqrt3/3]$ in every coordinate, inside $[0, s]$. Every axis of cube 8 has three non-zero coordinates, so it is turned in all four coordinates.

## 4. k = 18 to 25: side 2 + 1/√2

Let $s = 2 + 1/\sqrt2 = 2 + \sqrt2/2 \approx 2.7071068$. In the square $[0, s]^2$, 5 unit squares fit: the four corner squares $[0,1]^2$, $[s-1, s]\times[0,1]$, $[0,1]\times[s-1,s]$, $[s-1,s]^2$, and the square with centre $(s/2, s/2)$ turned $45°$, which is the diamond $|x - s/2| + |y - s/2| \le 1/\sqrt2$. The diamond meets each corner square only at a corner point such as $(1, 1)$, since $|1 - s/2| + |1 - s/2| = s - 2 = 1/\sqrt2$.

**Lemma (products).** If $S_1, \dots, S_5$ and $T_1, \dots, T_5$ are unit squares in $[0, s]^2$ with pairwise disjoint interiors, then the 25 products $S_a \times T_b$ are unit 4-cubes in $[0, s]^4$ with pairwise disjoint interiors.

*Proof.* The product of two unit squares, each turned in its respective plane, is a unit 4-cube whose frame is block diagonal, and it is contained in $[0, s]^2 \times [0, s]^2$. The interior of $S_a \times T_b$ is $\operatorname{int} S_a \times \operatorname{int} T_b$. For $(a, b) \ne (a', b')$, either $a \ne a'$ and the first factors have disjoint interiors, or $b \ne b'$ and the second factors do; in both cases the products have disjoint interiors. $\blacksquare$

So $s_4(25) \le 2 + 1/\sqrt2$. In the packing, 16 cubes are axis-parallel (corner × corner), 4 are turned $45°$ in $(x_3, x_4)$ (corner × centre), 4 in $(x_1, x_2)$ (centre × corner), and one in both planes (centre × centre). The certificate `n4k25.json` writes this packing in canonical form; the axis-parallel cubes keep the centres $\tfrac12$ and $s - \tfrac12$ in every coordinate, and the turned squares the centre $s/2 = 1 + \sqrt2/4$ in their plane.

The packings for $k = 18$ to $24$ are nested: each is obtained from the next larger packing by removing one cube, leaving all others unchanged. The removed cube is chosen according to the rule in `SPEC-ALG.md` (most rotated coordinate planes, then fewest contacts, then latest in index order). The rule counts rotated planes using the entries of the canonical frame: a plane $(x_p, x_q)$ is counted when $R_{pq}$ or $R_{qp}$ is neither $0$ nor $\pm1$. Thus, a cube turned $45°$ in one plane may contribute more than one counted plane depending on how its canonical frame orders the axes (`NESTING.md` lists these counts). Passing from 25 to 24 removes the cube turned in both planes, followed by one cube turned in a single plane at each step; the 16 axis-parallel cubes remain throughout. Because removing cubes preserves disjointness and containment, these certificates do not improve upon the bound $s_4(k) \le s_4(25)$, but they provide an explicit file for each $k$. The source (xavierdmello/tesseract-packing) similarly describes its packings for these values of $k$ as products of two 5-square packings.

**How the packing was obtained.** The published packing for $k = 29$ (xavierdmello/tesseract-packing, side $2.9428309369$, which refines hockyy/tesseract-packing's $2.942833710$) was the starting point. Its frames carry small tilts, up to about $13°$. We replaced every frame by the nearest signed permutation composed with $45°$ turns in one or two coordinate planes. Once the frames are fixed, the separation conditions along fixed candidate axes and the container conditions are linear in the centres, so with the side fixed at $2 + 2\sqrt2/3$ a linear programme finds centres; its optimal vertex is read back exactly in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and certified. The exact packing shows that the tilts are not needed. The centres in the file are those of the canonical form of §2: with the same frames and side, the cubes that can move are placed by the lexicographic linear programmes. The arrangement itself (the 25-cube product frame opened up, with cubes in “cross cells”, in the words of the source) is due to the authors of the published packings.

The packings for $k = 26$ to $28$ are nested subsets of the packing for $k = 29$, obtained by the same rule as in §4: from 29 to 28 cube 17 is removed, from 28 to 27 cube 16 (both turned in $(x_1, x_4)$), and from 27 to 26 cube 13 (turned in two planes). The packing for $k = 26$ therefore contains 15 axis-parallel cubes and 11 turned cubes. For $k = 26$, the source printed $2.942809042$ and conjectured the closed form $2 + 2\sqrt2/3$; our certificate confirms that this side length is attained.

## 6. k = 30: side 2.9876881

The packing for $k = 30$ has side $s = 2.9876880563\ldots$, the rational number in the certificate (a numerator and a denominator of 108 and 107 digits), below the published value $2.989568410$ by $0.0018804$. It is not in a quadratic field: the certificate (the rational format of the README of Release `cubes-n-dim-n4k30-v1`) gives each centre as rationals and each frame as the Cayley transform $R_i = (I - A_i)^{-1}(I + A_i)$ of a skew-symmetric rational matrix $A_i$, so that $R_i$ is orthogonal exactly; containment and the separation of every pair along the 56 candidate axes are then checked with exact rational arithmetic, as in §2. No frame is a signed permutation; four are within $10^{-6}$ of one.

**How the packing was obtained.** We initialized our search from the published packing for $k = 30$ (side length $2.989568410$, with identical coordinates in hockyy/tesseract-packing and xavierdmello/tesseract-packing). We fixed the container side and lowered it in decrements of $0.0005$. At each step, the centres and frames of the 30 cubes were moved to minimize a quadratic penalty on pairwise overlaps along the 56 candidate axes and on wall violations. At the third step ($s = 2.98807$), the frames changed substantially and the penalty vanished. We then tightened the packing and restored feasibility via sequential quadratic programming with the separating directions as variables, down to a floating-point side length of $2.98768826$. To construct the certificate, the Cayley parameters and centres were rounded to denominator $2^{40}$, after which pairwise separation was re-verified exactly and the side length was recomputed using exact rational arithmetic. The floating-point packing maintained a wall margin of $10^{-7}$, which this exact recomputation removes. The result is a distinct packing reached by continuation from the published one; credit for the initial packing belongs to its authors. Applying the same continuation procedure to the published packings for $k = 26$ to $29$ yielded no improvement.

## 7. Reproducing the checks

Release `cubes-n-dim-n4-certs-v3` and the folder [problems/cubes-n-dim/n4-certs/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-n-dim/n4-certs) contain the 13 certificates `certs/n4k17.json` to `certs/n4k29.json`, both checkers, `SPEC-ALG.md`, `NESTING.md` (listing the removed cubes), and `SHA256SUMS`. Using Python 3 with no external dependencies, execute from that folder:

```sh
# first checker: 13 × ACCEPT
python3 -I check_alg.py certs/n4k*.json
# second checker: 13 × ACCEPT
for f in certs/n4k*.json; do python3 -I check2/alg_check2.py "$f"; done
# canonical form
python3 -I check2/canon_check2.py certs/n4k*.json
# nesting and the removal rule
python3 -I check2/canon_check2.py --nest certs/n4k1[89].json certs/n4k2[0-5].json
python3 -I check2/canon_check2.py --nest certs/n4k2[6-9].json
```

For $k = 30$, execute from the folder [problems/cubes-n-dim/n4k30/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-n-dim/n4k30) (Release `cubes-n-dim-n4k30-v1`):

```sh
# first and second checker: ACCEPT
python3 -I check_cert.py certs/n4k30.json
python3 -I check2/cert_check2.py certs/n4k30.json
# corrupted copies are all rejected
python3 -I check2/neg_tests.py certs/n4k30.json
```

All packings for $k = 17$ to $30$ can be viewed, rotating in four dimensions, on the page [Unit 4-cubes in a 4-cube](cubes4-survey.html).
