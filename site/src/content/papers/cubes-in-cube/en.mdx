## Abstract

Let $s_3(n)$ be the side of the smallest cube that contains $n$ unit cubes with pairwise disjoint interiors, at any positions and orientations. We prove $s_3(9) = s_3(10) = 2 + 1/\sqrt2$. The upper bounds are the packings listed on Erich Friedman's Packing Center page "Cubes in Cubes": 8 corner cubes and one or two cubes turned $45^\circ$ about a vertical axis. We write them exactly in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and check them with two exact checkers; we claim no originality for them. The lower bound is new as far as we know. It rests on an 8-point lemma: with $s = 2 + 1/\sqrt2$, every closed unit cube in $[0, s]^3$ contains a point of $V = \{1, s-1\}^3$. A scaling argument then rules out 9 cubes in a smaller cube. The 8-point lemma is proved by an interval branch and bound over the six parameters of a pose, in which every box is closed by one of five rules proved here: a point test, a centre rule from Lemma F, a wall lemma, a pair lemma and a local sign lemma. The bound is attained, so the margin is zero at several poses; every rule is a closed inequality and no epsilon is used. The branch and bound was run by two independent checkers, both complete with 0 unresolved boxes. The proof is computer-assisted and has not been peer reviewed.

## Notation

- $s = 2 + 1/\sqrt2$, so $s - 2 = 1/\sqrt2$, and $V = \{1, s-1\}^3$ is a set of 8 points.
- A *pose* is a pair $(R, c)$ with $R \in SO(3)$ and $c \in \mathbb R^3$. Its cube is $Q = \{c + Rt : \lVert t\rVert_\infty \le \tfrac12\}$. The columns $a_0, a_1, a_2$ of $R$ are the cube's axes.
- For a point $p$, the local coordinates are $t_i(p) = a_i \cdot (p - c)$, and $p \in Q$ iff $|t_i(p)| \le \tfrac12$ for $i = 0, 1, 2$.
- $h_w = \tfrac12 \sum_j |R_{wj}|$, half the $\ell^1$ norm of row $w$ of $R$. $Q \subseteq [0, s]^3$ iff $h_w \le c_w \le s - h_w$ for every world axis $w$ (support function). Every row is a unit vector, so $\tfrac12 \le h_w \le \sqrt3/2 < s/2$.

## 1. Main lemma and the scaling argument

**Main lemma (8-point lemma).** Every closed unit cube $Q \subseteq [0, s]^3$ contains a point of $V$. A point on the boundary of $Q$ counts.

**Theorem.** $s_3(9) = s_3(10) = s$.

*Proof.* Upper bound: [upper/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-in-cube/upper) gives exact packings of 9 and 10 unit cubes in $[0, s]^3$, written in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$. Each is checked by two independent exact checkers. They are the packings listed on Erich Friedman's page ([Sources](#sources)).

Lower bound: suppose 9 unit cubes with pairwise disjoint interiors fit in $[0, L]^3$ with $L < s$. Then we can find 9 closed unit cubes in $[0, s]^3$ that are pairwise disjoint, as follows.

- Put $\lambda = L/s < 1$.
- Translate each cube by $(1/\lambda - 1)\, c_i$, where $c_i$ is its centre. The new centre is $c_i/\lambda$ and the orientation is unchanged.
- The translated cube lies in $[0, s]^3$. With $h_w$ the support values of the cube, the original cube satisfies $h_w \le c_{i,w} \le L - h_w$. Dividing by $\lambda$ gives
  $$
  h_w \le h_w/\lambda \le c_{i,w}/\lambda \le (L - h_w)/\lambda = s - h_w/\lambda \le s - h_w,
  $$
  which is the containment condition for the translated cube.
- Two cubes with disjoint interiors have a separating direction $u$ with $u\cdot(c_j - c_i) \ge \sigma_i(u) + \sigma_j(u) > 0$ ($\sigma$ the support values). After the translation the left side is multiplied by $1/\lambda > 1$, so the inequality becomes strict and the closed cubes are disjoint.

By the main lemma each of the 9 closed cubes contains a point of $V$. Since $|V| = 8$, two of them share a point, which contradicts disjointness. Hence $s_3(9) \ge s$, and $s_3(10) \ge s_3(9) \ge s$. $\blacksquare$

The rest of this paper proves the main lemma. A pose with $\lVert t\rVert_\infty \le \tfrac12$ at a point counts as containing it, and every inequality below is closed. The bound is attained (9 cubes fit at side $s$), so no strict margin can be bought; the zero-margin poses are handled exactly by the lemmas below.

Two independent exact checks accept all three packings:

- `check_q2.py` (no sympy; its own field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ on Python Fractions, with an exact sign test): containment by vertices; disjoint interiors, by computing the exact volume of $A \cap B$. Both reject the negative controls: two stacked rotated cubes overlapping by 0.4 in height, and a cube protruding through the floor (`check_q2.py`). As a sanity check of the volume routine, the cube with itself gives 1, a half-shifted copy gives $\tfrac12$, and the cube rotated $45^\circ$ about its own centre gives $2(\sqrt2 - 1)$.

## 2. Lemma F (centre lemma)

**Lemma F.** For every $R \in SO(3)$ and every column $k$, $F_k(R) := \sum_w |R_{wk}|\,(2 - 2h_w) \le 1$.

This is the inequality behind $s_3(2) = 2$. The proof (§2.2, §2.3) is a hand proof when some $|R_{wk}| \ge 1/\sqrt2$, and a computer proof elsewhere, by two independent exact or interval checkers.

**Corollary (centre rule).** If $|p_w - c_w| \le 1 - h_w$ for every $w$, then $p \in Q$.

*Proof.* $|t_i(p)| \le \sum_w |R_{wi}|\,|p_w - c_w| \le \sum_w |R_{wi}|(1 - h_w) = \tfrac12 F_i(R) \le \tfrac12$. $\blacksquare$

At the wall $p_w = 1$ with $h_w \le c_w \le 1$, the condition reads $1 - c_w \le 1 - h_w$, which always holds. The checkers use the parametrisation $c_w = h_w + u_w(s - 2h_w)$, $u_w \in [0, 1]$, which keeps this exact under interval arithmetic.

### 2.1 Lemma F and s₃(2) = 2

In this section write $a_{ik} = |R_{ik}|$ and $w_i = \sum_j |R_{ij}|$ (the $\ell^1$ norm of row $i$, i.e. the width of the cube $R[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^3$ in direction $e_i$; $w_i = 2h_i$), so that $F_k(R) = \sum_i a_{ik}(2 - w_i)$. Lemma F is symmetric under signs and under permutations of rows and columns, so it may be stated for $R \in O(3)$. The proof below uses only orthogonality.

**Corollary ($s_3(2) = 2$).** Let $a < 2$. If a unit cube $C = c + R[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^3$ lies in $K_a = [-a/2, a/2]^3$, then the centre 0 of $K_a$ lies in the interior of $C$. Hence two unit cubes with disjoint interiors do not fit in a cube of side $a < 2$. With monotonicity and the $2\times2\times2$ packing, $s_3(n) = 2$ for $2 \le n \le 8$.

*Proof of the corollary.*

- Suppose 0 is not in the interior of $C$. Since $C = \{x : \lVert R^{\mathsf T}(x - c)\rVert_\infty \le \tfrac12\}$, some column $u_k$ of $R$ has $|u_k\cdot c| \ge \tfrac12$.
- Containment means $|c_i| + w_i/2 \le a/2$ for every $i$, so $a - w_i \ge 0$.
- Hence $|u_k\cdot c| \le \sum_i a_{ik}|c_i| \le \sum_i a_{ik}(a - w_i)/2 < \sum_i a_{ik}(2 - w_i)/2 = F_k/2$. The inequality is strict because $a < 2$ and $\sum_i a_{ik} \ge 1$.
- By Lemma F, $|u_k\cdot c| < \tfrac12$, a contradiction. $\blacksquare$

We claim no novelty for $s_3(n) = 2$, $2 \le n \le 8$; it is included because the main proof uses Lemma F.

### 2.2 Near an axis: if some |R_ik| ≥ 1/√2, then F_k ≤ 1 (hand proof)

By permuting and negating rows and columns, take $k = 1$, $i = 1$ and $R_{11} = \cos\theta \ge 1/\sqrt2$, with $0 \le \theta \le \pi/4$. Let $x = (R_{21}, R_{31})$ (so $\lVert x\rVert_2 = \sin\theta$), let $M$ be the lower-right $2\times2$ block, and let $u$ be column 1.

(a) Since $\sum_i a_{i1}w_i = 1 + \sum_i a_{i1}v_i$ with $v_i = w_i - a_{i1}$,
$$
1 - F_1 = 2(1 - \cos\theta) - 2\lVert x\rVert_1 + a_{11}(|R_{12}| + |R_{13}|) + \sum_{i=2,3} |x_i|\,\lVert M_i\rVert_1.
$$

(b) Columns 1 and $j$ are orthogonal: $R_{11}R_{1j} + (M^{\mathsf T}x)_j = 0$. Hence $a_{11}(|R_{12}| + |R_{13}|) = \lVert M^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1$.

(c) Polar decomposition of $M$.

- Rows 2 and 3 are orthonormal, so $MM^{\mathsf T} = I - xx^{\mathsf T}$.
- The polar decomposition is $M = PQ$ with $P = (I - xx^{\mathsf T})^{1/2} = I - (1 - \cos\theta)\,\hat x\hat x^{\mathsf T}$, where $\hat x = x/\sin\theta$. $P$ is invertible because its eigenvalues are 1 and $\cos\theta > 0$, so $Q = P^{-1}M$ is orthogonal.
- $Px = \cos\theta\cdot x$, so $M^{\mathsf T}x = \cos\theta\cdot Q^{\mathsf T}x$.
- Row $i$ of $M$ is $Q_i - (1 - \cos\theta)\,\hat x_i(\hat x^{\mathsf T}Q)$. Using $\sum_i |x_i||\hat x_i| = \sin\theta$, this gives
  $$
  \sum_i |x_i|\,\lVert M_i\rVert_1 \ge \sum_i |x_i|\,\lVert Q_i\rVert_1 - (1 - \cos\theta)\,\lVert Q^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1.
  $$

(d) Together:
$$
1 - F_1 \ge K_2 + 2(1 - \cos\theta)(1 - \lVert Q^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1), \qquad K_2 = \lVert Q^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1 + \sum_i |x_i|\,\lVert Q_i\rVert_1 - 2\lVert x\rVert_1.
$$

(e) $K_2 \ge 0$ (the planar case).

- Every row and column of $Q$ has $\ell^1$ norm $L = |\cos\varphi| + |\sin\varphi| \in [1, \sqrt2]$.
- The $\ell^1$ operator norm of $Q$ is its largest column $\ell^1$ norm, so $\lVert x\rVert_1 = \lVert Q(Q^{\mathsf T}x)\rVert_1 \le L\,\lVert Q^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1$.
- Hence $K_2 \ge \lVert x\rVert_1(1/L + L - 2) = \lVert x\rVert_1(L - 1)^2/L \ge 0$.

(f) $\lVert Q^{\mathsf T}x\rVert_1 \le \sqrt2\,\lVert x\rVert_2 = \sqrt2\sin\theta \le 1$, because $\theta \le \pi/4$.

So $1 - F_1 \ge 2(1 - \cos\theta)(1 - \sqrt2\sin\theta) \ge 0$. $\blacksquare$

Equality $F_k = 1$ holds at $\theta = 0$, i.e. when the column is a coordinate axis. Away from it the margin grows quadratically. This zero margin is the reason the near-axis part is proved by hand.

### 2.3 Far from the axes: if every |R_ik| ≤ 1/√2, then F_k < 1 (computer proof)

Two independent checkers cover $SO(3)$ and, for every box and column, close the box if either some $|R_{ik}|^2 > \tfrac12$ throughout the box (§2.2 applies), or $F_k < 1$ throughout the box. Otherwise the box is split.

## 3. Wall lemma

**Wall lemma.** Let $p \in V$ with $p_w = 1$, and assume $c_w \ge h_w$ (lower wall). Suppose that for an axis $i$ and a sign $\sigma$ we have $\sigma R_{wi} > 0$ and $|R_{wj}| + |t_j(p)| \le \tfrac12$ for both $j \ne i$. Then $\sigma t_i(p) \le \tfrac12$.

*Proof.* Row $w$ of $p - c = Rt$ gives $\sum_j R_{wj}t_j = 1 - c_w \le 1 - h_w$. Put $\rho = |R_{wi}| = \sigma R_{wi} > 0$. Then
$$
\rho\cdot\sigma t_i = R_{wi}t_i \le 1 - h_w + \sum_{j\ne i} |R_{wj}||t_j| = 1 - \tfrac12\rho - \sum_{j\ne i} |R_{wj}|(\tfrac12 - |t_j|).
$$
By hypothesis $\tfrac12 - |t_j| \ge |R_{wj}|$, so the last sum is at least $\sum_{j\ne i} R_{wj}^2 = 1 - \rho^2$. Hence $\rho\cdot\sigma t_i \le \rho^2 - \tfrac12\rho$, so $\sigma t_i \le \rho - \tfrac12 \le \tfrac12$. $\blacksquare$

The upper wall ($p_w = s - 1$, $c_w \le s - h_w$) is the mirror image $c \mapsto s - c$.

**Tightening at a wall.** If $p_w = 1$, $c_w \ge h_w$ and $R_{wk} \ge 0$, then
$$
R_{wk}(p_w - c_w) \le R_{wk}(1 - h_w) = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12(1 - R_{wk})^2 - \tfrac12 R_{wk}\sigma_{wk}, \qquad \sigma_{wk} = \sum_{j\ne k} |R_{wj}|.
$$
The identity follows from $2h_w = R_{wk} + \sigma_{wk}$. The bound replaces the $w$-term of the upper bound of $t_k(p)$ and avoids interval dependency. The case $R_{wk} \le 0$ (lower bound) and the upper wall are mirror images.

## 4. Pair lemma

Let $p, q \in V$ differ in exactly two coordinates. Then $d = q - p$ has two entries $\pm1/\sqrt2$, so $|d| = 1$, and there are 12 such pairs. We have $t_i(q) = t_i(p) + a_i\cdot d$, and $|a_i\cdot d| \le 1$.

**Pair lemma.** Suppose that for some axis $i$ and sign $\sigma$:

- both points satisfy $|t_j| \le \tfrac12$ for both $j \ne i$;
- $\sigma t_i(p) \le \tfrac12$;
- $\sigma t_i(q) \ge -\tfrac12$.

Then $p \in Q$ or $q \in Q$.

*Proof.* If $\sigma t_i(p) \ge -\tfrac12$, then $p \in Q$. Otherwise $\sigma t_i(q) \le \sigma t_i(p) + 1 < \tfrac12$, so $q \in Q$. $\blacksquare$

## 5. Local sign lemma

The checkers do not close one pose by the rules above: the cube rotated $45^\circ$ about the $x$ axis, touching the wall $x = 0$, with centre $(\tfrac12, s/2, s/2)$. Its four points with $x = 1$ lie at the midpoints of the edges of its face $x = 1$. Near this pose, which of them is contained switches with the sign of two continuous quantities. No box around the pose can therefore be closed by naming one point. The following lemma names two points and closes a whole neighbourhood, including the pose itself.

Notation:

- $g = (1, s/2, s/2)$, the centre of the four points with $x = 1$;
- the four points are $q_\sigma = g + a(0, \sigma_y, \sigma_z)$, with $\sigma \in \{\pm1\}^2$ and $a = s/2 - 1 = 1/(2\sqrt2)$;
- $f = c + \tfrac12 a_0$, the centre of the far face;
- $e = g - f$, $p = a_1\cdot e$ and $r = a_2\cdot e$;
- $\nu = (a_{0y}, a_{0z})$ and $d = (s/2 - c_y, s/2 - c_z)$.

**Lemma L.** Assume all of the following:

- $c_x \ge h_x$ and $a_{0x} > 0$;
- $\lVert d\rVert_2 < \tfrac12 a_{0x} - a$;
- $a_{1y} + a_{1z} \ge 0$ and $a_{2z} - a_{2y} \ge 0$;
- $|p| \le \tfrac12$ and $|r| \le \tfrac12$;
- $|r| + a|a_{2y} + a_{2z}| \le \tfrac12$ and $|p| + a|a_{1y} - a_{1z}| \le \tfrac12$;
- $t_0(q_\sigma) \ge -\tfrac12$ for all four $\sigma$.

Put $\varsigma_A = -\operatorname{sgn} p$ and $\varsigma_B = \operatorname{sgn} r$ (either sign if $p = 0$ or $r = 0$). Then $A = q_{(\varsigma_A, \varsigma_A)}$ or $B = q_{(\varsigma_B, -\varsigma_B)}$ lies in $Q$.

*Proof.* For a point $q$, $q \in Q$ iff $t_0(q) \ge -\tfrac12$ and the following three conditions hold:

- (i) $a_0\cdot(q - f) \le 0$, i.e. $t_0(q) \le \tfrac12$;
- (ii) $|a_1\cdot(q - f)| \le \tfrac12$;
- (iii) $|a_2\cdot(q - f)| \le \tfrac12$.

The first condition, $t_0(q) \ge -\tfrac12$, is a hypothesis.

1. *(ii) and (iii) by signs.*
   - $a_1\cdot(A - f) = p + \varsigma_A a(a_{1y} + a_{1z})$. Here $a|a_{1y} + a_{1z}| \le a\sqrt2 = \tfrac12$, and the two terms have opposite signs, so $|a_1\cdot(A - f)| \le \max(|p|, \tfrac12) \le \tfrac12$.
   - $|a_2\cdot(A - f)| \le |r| + a|a_{2y} + a_{2z}| \le \tfrac12$.
   - For $B$ in the same way: $a_2\cdot(B - f) = r - \varsigma_B a(a_{2z} - a_{2y})$, whose two terms have opposite signs, and $|a_1\cdot(B - f)| \le |p| + a|a_{1y} - a_{1z}| \le \tfrac12$.
2. *(i) from the wall.*
   - $a_0\cdot e = a_{0x}(1 - c_x) + \nu\cdot d - \tfrac12$.
   - From $c_x \ge h_x$ and the identity $a_{0x}(1 - h_x) = \tfrac12 - \tfrac12(1 - a_{0x})^2 - \tfrac12 a_{0x}\sigma_x$, with $\sigma_x = |a_{1x}| + |a_{2x}|$, we get $a_0\cdot e \le \nu\cdot d - \tfrac12 a_{0x}\sigma_x - \tfrac12(1 - a_{0x})^2$.
   - Row $x$ and column 0 of $R$ are unit vectors, so $a_{1x}^2 + a_{2x}^2 = 1 - a_{0x}^2 = \lVert\nu\rVert^2$, and hence $\sigma_x \ge \lVert\nu\rVert$.
   - Since $a_0\cdot(q_\sigma - f) = a_0\cdot e + a\,\nu\cdot\sigma$, condition (i) for $q_\sigma$ follows from $\nu\cdot d + a\,\nu\cdot\sigma \le \tfrac12 a_{0x}\lVert\nu\rVert$.
3. *A and B cannot both fail (i).*
   - We have $\nu\cdot\sigma_A = \varsigma_A(\nu_y + \nu_z)$ and $\nu\cdot\sigma_B = \varsigma_B(\nu_y - \nu_z)$.
   - The signs $\varsigma_A$ and $\varsigma_B$ were fixed in step 1 by $p$ and $r$, not by $\nu$, so the signs of these two numbers are not known. We therefore use only the bounds $\nu\cdot\sigma_A \le |\nu_y + \nu_z|$ and $\nu\cdot\sigma_B \le |\nu_y - \nu_z|$, which hold for either sign.
   - Suppose the sufficient condition of step 2 fails for both $A$ and $B$. By these bounds, $\nu\cdot d + a|\nu_y + \nu_z| > \tfrac12 a_{0x}\lVert\nu\rVert$ and $\nu\cdot d + a|\nu_y - \nu_z| > \tfrac12 a_{0x}\lVert\nu\rVert$. Hence $\nu\cdot d + a\cdot\min(|\nu_y + \nu_z|, |\nu_y - \nu_z|) > \tfrac12 a_{0x}\lVert\nu\rVert$.
   - The minimum is at most $\sqrt{((\nu_y + \nu_z)^2 + (\nu_y - \nu_z)^2)/2} = \lVert\nu\rVert$, so $\nu\cdot d > (\tfrac12 a_{0x} - a)\lVert\nu\rVert$.
   - But $\nu\cdot d \le \lVert\nu\rVert\lVert d\rVert < (\tfrac12 a_{0x} - a)\lVert\nu\rVert$, a contradiction when $\nu \ne 0$.
   - If $\nu = 0$, then $a_{0x} = 1$ and $\sigma_x = 0$, so (i) holds for every $\sigma$. $\blacksquare$

*Remarks.*

- The proof uses only that $(a_0, a_1, a_2)$ is an orthonormal frame with $Q = \{c + \sum t_k a_k : |t_k| \le \tfrac12\}$. Orientation is never used, so the lemma applies to any signed permutation of the columns of $R$.
- The images of the lemma under the symmetries of the container that fix $V$ (coordinate permutations and reflections $c \mapsto s - c$) hold as well.
- At the pose itself, $p = r = 0$, $A$ lies on an edge of $Q$ and (i) holds with equality. Since $Q$ is closed, $A \in Q$.

## 6. Computer part

The main lemma is reduced to finitely many interval inequalities over a 6-parameter domain (rotation and centre). Two independent checkers cover the whole domain, using §2 to §5 as closing rules. Each box is closed by one rule, or split. Both use the centre parametrisation $c_w = h_w + u_w(s - 2h_w)$ with $u \in [0, 1]^3$, which is exactly the set of feasible centres, including all wall, edge and corner contacts.

| | First checker ([checker/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-in-cube/checker)) | Second checker ([checker2/](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/cubes-in-cube/checker2)) |
|---|---|---|
| Rotation | projective quaternion $(1, x, y, z)$, $R = N(q)/(1 + x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$ | ZYZ Euler angles, $R = R_z(\alpha)R_y(\beta)R_z(\gamma)$ |
| Symmetry reduction | 1,152-fold: the 24 rotations of the cube and the 48 symmetries of the container | 32-fold: the 8 reflections of the container and a quarter turn about one cube axis |
| Root box | $[0, a]^2 \times [-a, a] \times [0, \tfrac12] \times [0, 1]^2$, $a$ an upper bound for $\sqrt2 - 1$ | $[0, 2\pi] \times [0, \pi] \times [0, \pi/2] \times [0, \tfrac12]^3$, angle endpoints enclosed outward |
| Arithmetic | every interval operation widened by `next_down`/`next_up`; all constants enclosed | its own interval type, rounded one step outward at each endpoint; its own interval sine and cosine (Taylor polynomials with a remainder bound) |
| Local sign lemma | rule `local_ab` | rule `local`, in all 288 forms |

The second checker was written from the statements of the lemmas only, without reading the first. The closing rules are:

| Rule | Closes a box when |
|---|---|
| point | some $p \in V$ has $|t_i(p)| \le \tfrac12$ for $i = 0, 1, 2$ on the whole box |
| centre | centre rule (§2), in the collected form $2 - u_w s - 2h_w(1 - u_w) \ge 0$ (mirror for $p_w = s - 1$) |
| wall | wall lemma (§3), both walls; the bounds on $t$ use the wall tightening |
| pair | pair lemma (§4) for the 12 pairs at distance 1 |
| local | local sign lemma L (§5); all hypotheses are evaluated over the box, including $t_0 \ge -\tfrac12$ at the four $x = 1$ points |

No epsilon or tolerance appears in any closing inequality. Each checker writes a receipt with a chained SHA-256 digest over the leaf stream: starting from sha256(""), after every round $h \leftarrow$ sha256($h$ ‖ the round's leaf records). Both searches are deterministic; the digest does not depend on the thread count or on stopping and resuming.

- First checker: 100,000 random poses (20,000 at walls), with the witness of each closing box checked in floating point, 0 false closures; 9,356 boxes of radius $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-2}$ near the $45^\circ$ wall pose, of which 4,962 close by `local_ab` and 248,100 poses sampled in them all contain $A$ or $B$; 863,544 poses sampled from the leaf stream, 0 failures, the worst excess 0.0, which is equality at the $45^\circ$ pose.
- Second checker: 100,000 random poses and 20,000 poses at walls, 0 false closures; each rule alone on 20,000 further boxes; 432 boxes around the images of the $45^\circ$ wall pose, all closed by `local`; 20,000 random boxes with 1,487,928 sampled poses and 6,000 boxes around the $45^\circ$ wall pose with 579,488 sampled poses, 0 failures; determinism and resume tests.

## Status and limitations

- A computer-assisted proof, checked by two independent checkers. It has not been peer reviewed and is not formalised in Lean.
- Friedman's page lists the packings for $n = 9$ and $n = 10$ at side $2 + 1/\sqrt2$, with no proof of optimality. We found no published proof of a lower bound $s_3(9) \ge 2 + 1/\sqrt2$; if you know of one, please open an issue.

## Sources

Our results build on three layers of earlier work:

- **Classical.** The point-set method is the standard lower-bound technique for packing unit squares in squares, described in Erich Friedman's survey [[1]](#ref-1). The packings of 9 and 10 cubes at side $2 + 1/\sqrt2$ are listed on Erich Friedman's Packing Center page "Cubes in Cubes" [[2]](#ref-2). As far as we found, no proof of their optimality has been published.
- **Our earlier lemmas.** These come from our work on $s_3(2) = 2$ and on $s_3(k^3 - 1) = k$: Lemma F (§2), the wall lemma (§3) and the pair lemma (§4).
- **New here.** The 8-point main lemma, the local sign lemma (§5), and the two checkers.
