## Abstract

Let $s(n)$ be the leg length of the smallest right isosceles triangle containing $n$ non-overlapping unit squares at arbitrary positions and orientations. We prove that $s(2) = 2\sqrt2$, $s(3) = 3$, $s(4) = 5/\sqrt2$, $s(6) = 4$, $s(10) = 5$, $s(15) = 6$, and $s(21) = 7$. The upper bounds are given by our packing certificates with exact coordinates, verified by two independent exact checkers; the packings are those listed in Erich Friedman's Packing Center, and we claim no originality for them. The lower-bound certificates establish that these packings are optimal. Each lower bound consists of a finite weighted point set such that every unit square in the triangle captures weight at least 1, while the total weight is strictly less than $n$. All arithmetic is exact, carried out in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, and every lower-bound certificate is verified by two independent checkers using different methods. For $n = 15$ and $21$, the optimal packing places certificate points on the edges of its squares; the certificates and both checkers handle these degenerate poses (§3.1, §3.2, §5). The results are computer-assisted and have not been peer reviewed.

## 1. The problem and the packings (upper bounds)

Let $T_v = \operatorname{conv}\{(0,0), (v,0), (0,v)\}$, with its right angle at the origin and its legs along the coordinate axes; $T_L$ is the same triangle with legs $L$. Every right isosceles triangle with legs $v$ is congruent to $T_v$, and a congruence maps a packing to a packing, so it suffices to consider $T_v$. The upper bounds are realized by the following packings.

Each upper bound is given by a packing certificate `certificates/nX/packing.json` specifying the container and the $n$ squares with exact coordinates (in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ for $n = 2, 4$, and rational otherwise). Two programs sharing no code verify in exact arithmetic that the container has the claimed shape and size, that each square is a closed unit square lying in the closed container, and that no two squares have overlapping interiors (contact along edges or at corners is permitted). Because the coordinates are algebraic and contacts correspond to exact equalities, floating-point arithmetic is insufficient. The verification relies on no external tables.

The packings are those listed in Erich Friedman's Packing Center under "Squares in Right Isosceles Triangles", where the cases $n = 2, 3, 6, 10, 15$, and $21$ are marked "Trivial" and the packing for $n = 4$ was found by Erich Friedman in March 2005; we claim no originality for them. Friedman's table provides the packings but gives no proofs or lower bounds; there, "Trivial" indicates that the construction is obvious, not that its optimality has been established.

## 2. The method: weighted point sets

Each lower bound is defined by a finite set of points $p_i \in T_v$ with rational weights $w_i \ge 0$, where $v$ is the claimed value. The checkers verify two conditions:

1. **Capture.** Every closed unit square $Q \subseteq T_v$, at any position and orientation, satisfies $$ \sum_{p_i \in Q} w_i \ge 1. $$ Points on the boundary of $Q$ are included. This condition is verified directly on the container with zero margin.
2. **Budget.** The total weight satisfies $\sum_i w_i < n$.

**Centre scaling.** It follows that $n$ squares cannot fit in a triangle with legs $L < v$. Suppose for contradiction that they did, with disjoint interiors, inside $T' = G + \lambda(T_v - G)$, a translate of $T_L$, where $\lambda = L/v$ and $G$ is any point in $T_v$. Translate each square by $(1/\lambda - 1)(c_i - G)$, where $c_i$ denotes its centre. For a point $k$ of the centred square, the point $c_i + k$ moves to $x' = G + \frac{c_i - G}{\lambda} + k$, and

$$
x' - G = \frac{c_i - G}{\lambda} + k = \lambda\cdot\frac{c_i + k - G}{\lambda} + (1-\lambda)\cdot\frac{c_i - G}{\lambda},
$$

and both fractions lie in $T_v - G$ because $c_i + k$ and $c_i$ belong to $T'$. Since $T_v - G$ is convex, $x' - G \in T_v - G$, that is, $x' \in T_v$; so the translated squares lie in $T_v$. They remain pairwise disjoint closed squares: for a separating direction $w$ satisfying $w\cdot(c_j - c_i) \ge h_i + h_j > 0$ (with $h_i$, $h_j$ the support values of the two centred squares in the direction $w$), the quantity $w\cdot(c_j - c_i)$ is scaled by $1/\lambda > 1$. Each square captures weight $\ge 1$, so the total captured weight would be $\ge n$, contradicting the budget. Together with the packing of leg length $v$, this proves $s(n) = v$.

**Exact arithmetic in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$.** Lengths and coordinates are elements $a + b\sqrt2$ with rational $a, b$. Their signs are determined exactly without rounding: if $a$ and $b$ are both $\ge 0$ (or both $\le 0$), the sign is their common sign, and the value is $0$ if and only if $a = b = 0$. Otherwise, the term with the larger absolute value determines the sign: $a + b\sqrt2$ has the sign of $a$ when $a^2 > 2b^2$, and the sign of $b$ when $a^2 < 2b^2$ (the case $a^2 = 2b^2$ is impossible since $\sqrt2$ is irrational). A square is contained in $T_L$ if and only if its four vertices satisfy $y \ge 0$, $x \ge 0$, and $x + y \le L$. Orientations are parametrised by $u = \tan(\theta/2)$. The reflection $(x, y) \mapsto (y, x)$ is the only nontrivial symmetry of $T_L$, mapping a square at angle $\theta$ to one at angle $\pi/2 - \theta$. Hence, for a reflection-invariant certificate, restricting to $\theta \in [0, \pi/4]$—that is, $u \in [0, \sqrt2 - 1]$—suffices, where $\sqrt2 - 1 = \tan(\pi/8)$ is exact in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$.

**Pairs at distance 1 (Lemma P, used for $n = 4, 6, 10, 15, 21$).** For two points $p, q$ at distance exactly 1, any square whose chord along the line $pq$ crosses two opposite edges and intersects the segment $[p, q]$ contains $p$ or $q$. This resolves poses where the captured point switches between $p$ and $q$ across a surface that is not parallel to the coordinate axes, such as a square resting at the midpoint of the hypotenuse for $n = 4$. The pair contributes $\min(w_p, w_q)$, and no point is counted twice. Lemma P is used only by the first checker; the second checker evaluates points exactly in every cell and does not require it.

All seven point sets are invariant under $(x, y) \mapsto (y, x)$.

- $n = 2$: the single point $(5\sqrt2/8, 5\sqrt2/8)$ of weight 1, located on the axis of symmetry at distance $3/4$ from the hypotenuse. Every closed unit square in $T_{2\sqrt2}$ contains it.
- $n = 3$: four points $(3/4, 1)$, $(1, 3/4)$, $(3/4, 5/4)$, $(5/4, 3/4)$ of weight $1/2$; every closed unit square in $T_3$ contains at least two of them.
- $n = 4$: three points of weight 1: $(\sqrt2/2, \sqrt2/2)$, the centre of the top square in the packing, and $(5\sqrt2/8, 9\sqrt2/8)$, $(9\sqrt2/8, 5\sqrt2/8)$, at distance $3/4$ from the hypotenuse along the boundaries between the bottom squares. The last two points are at distance exactly 1 and form a pair. Without this pair the first checker cannot terminate: near the midpoint of the hypotenuse at $\theta \approx 45^\circ$, the square switches between the two points across the diagonal $x = y$, which is not a face of the axis-parallel boxes.
- $n = 6$: points $(1/2, 3/2)$, $(3/2, 1/2)$, $(3/4, 1)$, $(1, 3/4)$ of weight $1/2$, and $(3/4, 2)$, $(2, 3/4)$, $(3/2, 3/2)$ of weight 1. There are four pairs: $(1/2,3/2)$–$(3/2,3/2)$, $(3/2,1/2)$–$(3/2,3/2)$, $(3/4,1)$–$(3/4,2)$, and $(1,3/4)$–$(2,3/4)$. Without them, the first checker leaves 5,677 uncertified boxes at depth 22, all near centre $(1, 1.3)$ at small angles.
- $n = 10$: points $(3/4, 1)$, $(1, 3/4)$ of weight $1/2$; points $(3/4, 2)$, $(2, 3/4)$, $(3/4, 3)$, $(3, 3/4)$ of weight 1; and points $(1, 3/2)$, $(3/2, 1)$, $(5/4, 5/2)$, $(5/2, 5/4)$, $(3/2, 3/2)$, $(3/2, 5/2)$, $(5/2, 3/2)$, $(7/4, 2)$, $(2, 7/4)$ of weight $1/2$. There are eight pairs (all point pairs at distance exactly 1). The root grid of the first checker is chosen over $[0, 6]^2$ so that the lines $x = 3/2$ and $y = 3/2$ lie on box faces: at centre $(3/2, 3/2)$ and $\theta = 0$, the square $[1,2]^2$ has four points on its boundary, and the captured point changes across these lines. Under the default bounding box, two boxes there remain uncertified at depth 22.
- $n = 15$: 505 points with rational coordinates, total weight $1499/100$, and 152 pairs (all point pairs at distance exactly 1). This certificate requires more infrastructure than the smaller cases; see §3.1.
- $n = 21$: 503 points with rational coordinates, total weight $2099/100$, and 418 pairs. See §3.2.

### 3.1 The case $n = 15$

The optimal staircase configuration is degenerate for a point certificate: at $\theta = 0$, squares of the packing contain certificate points exactly on their edges. Consequently, near these poses, which points are captured depends on the direction of movement. The certificate addresses three types of such poses:

- **Slabs** (centre on $x = k + 1/2$ or $y = j + 1/2$, $\theta = 0$): by Lemma P, at least one of the two points $(k, s)$ or $(k + 1, s)$ is contained in the square.
- **Lattice crossings** (centre $(k + 1/2, j + 1/2)$, $\theta = 0$, moving in both directions simultaneously): the weights were obtained via a floating-point LP with one constraint per quarter-box around each crossing and per slab height. The LP counts only features verifiable by the first checker over the entire box: interior points, edge points that remain inside, and pairs weighted by $\min(w_p, w_q)$. Points off the integer lines were then perturbed by at most $1/200$, the weights were scaled to sum to $14.99$, and all unit-distance pairs were included.
- **A wedged square**: the square $[0, 1] \times [4, 5]$ at $\theta = 0$ touches both a leg and the hypotenuse. Three constraint lines intersect there at the boundary of the angular range; the first checker certifies the surrounding boxes using open-end leaves (§5).

The first checker uses a $32 \times 32$ root grid over $[0, 8]^2$, aligning integer and half-integer lines with box faces. The second checker evaluates points exactly at every pose and requires none of these adjustments.

### 3.2 The case $n = 21$

The certificate has the same structure as for $n = 15$: a float LP with rows for the degenerate poses of the staircase, here on the $1/8$ grid, alternated with a float branch and bound over all poses until it found no deficit pose. Then float screens of the degenerate poses at $\theta = 0$ and at the poses with three points on edges, and small LPs that only add weight there, with the total scaled back to $20.99$. The margin is smaller (total $20.99$ against $21$), and the first checker needs two more tools, both described in [checker/SPEC.md](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/right-isosceles/checker/SPEC.md).

- **Cone leaves** (`--cone 1/16`). At $\theta = 0$, a square may have a vertex on the hypotenuse and its remaining two edges along lines of the $1/8$ grid. Near such a pose, point containment depends on the direction of perturbation (the ratio of the centre displacement to $\theta$); standard subdivision therefore never terminates on boxes touching the pose. Cone leaves partition the box into pieces according to the centre's offsets $|x - X|$ and $|y - Y|$ from a corner $(X, Y)$ of the box, each divided by $g = u/(1 + u^2)$.
- **Two-point lemma.** Consider two points, each having at most two containment conditions unverified on a box. If for every pair $(C_1, C_2)$ of these open conditions there exists $\lambda(u) \in [0, 1]$ (constant, $\alpha u$, or $1 - \alpha u$) such that $\lambda C_1 + (1 - \lambda) C_2 \ge 0$ at every vertex of the polygon of the box's admissible centres, for every $u$ of the box (the conditions are affine in the centre), then at least one of the two points is contained in the square at every pose in the box, contributing $\min(w_1, w_2)$. This lemma is needed for $[33/8, 41/8] \times [0, 1]$ at $\theta = 0$, flush with the leg $y = 0$: here $(41/8, 1/2)$ exits across the right edge while $(33/8, 1)$ exits across the top edge. The transition between them is of second order in $\theta$, so Lemma P alone cannot verify the surrounding boxes.

The root grid is identical to that used for $n = 15$. The second checker evaluates points exactly at every pose and requires neither tool.

## 4. Conclusion

For each $n \in \{2, 3, 4, 6, 10, 15, 21\}$, the certificate captures weight $\ge 1$ within every closed unit square in $T_v$ and has total weight $< n$. By centre scaling (§2), $s(n) \ge v$, while the packing in §1 establishes $s(n) \le v$. Hence

$$
s(2) = 2\sqrt2, \quad s(3) = 3, \quad s(4) = \frac{5}{\sqrt2}, \quad s(6) = 4, \quad s(10) = 5, \quad s(15) = 6, \quad s(21) = 7. \qquad \blacksquare
$$

## 5. Verification

Each lower-bound certificate is verified by two checkers employing different methods.

- **Checker 1** subdivides pose space (centre box $\times$ angle bin, with $u = \tan(\theta/2)$) with Bernstein bounds, uses the reflection symmetry and Lemma P, and supports contacts with margin zero. With the option `--slices` (used for $n = 15$ and $21$) it also certifies boxes at the ends of the angle range, by endpoint slices (the admissible centres at the end angle form a polygon with exact vertices) and by open-end leaves. With `--cone` (used for $n = 21$) it also uses cone leaves; the two-point lemma (§3.2) is always on, on cone pieces and on boxes at the end $u = 0$ of the angle range. It is adapted from our checker for squares in an equilateral triangle ([problems/triangle](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/triangle/README.md)), with the container, the number field ($\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ instead of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$) and the symmetry changed; the changes are listed in [checker/SPEC.md](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/right-isosceles/checker/SPEC.md), and the soundness argument is in that checker's [README](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/triangle/checker/README.md). Standard library only.
- **Checker 2** counts the points exactly, by an angular sweep. The critical angles are the real roots of the pair and triple determinants of the line arrangement, each open gap between them gets one rational sample, and at each sample the capture weight is counted exactly on every open sector at every vertex inside the admissible region. Closure is by upper semicontinuity; it needs a disc of centres inside every admissible region. The inradius $r = L(2-\sqrt2)/2$ of the container $T_L$ exceeds the circumradius $\sqrt2/2$ of the unit square exactly when $L > 1 + \sqrt2$; then at every angle the admissible centres contain the disc of radius $r - \sqrt2/2$ about the incentre of $T_L$. The condition $L > 1 + \sqrt2$ is checked exactly.
  - For $n = 2$ to $10$ (`sweep_q2.py`) every angle $u \in [0, 1]$ is swept over the whole arrangement, without box subdivision.
  - For $n = 15$ and $21$ (`local_sweep.py`) a sweep of the whole arrangement is out of reach: for $n = 15$ the 505 points give about 2,000 lines and $1.4 \times 10^9$ triples. The checker runs a branch and bound over boxes in pose space ($u$ and the centre; 20 slices in $u$ times a $16 \times 16$ grid over $[0, 6]^2$ for $n = 15$ and over $[0, 7]^2$ for $n = 21$, 5,120 root boxes). With exact interval arithmetic a box is closed as inadmissible (some vertex of $Q$ is outside a side of $T_L$ at every pose of the box), as sure (the points inside $Q$ at every pose of the box weigh $\ge 1$), or, when at most 24 points change membership in it, by the same sweep restricted to the box and to the lines that matter there; otherwise it is split.

  It uses no Bernstein bounds, no symmetry and no Lemma P. It was written from the equilateral-triangle specification, the certificates and their proof notes only; the first checker's code was not opened, and the two checkers share only the certificate format. It uses gmpy2 (exact rationals) and sympy (real-root isolation). Centre scaling is in the proof notes and is not re-checked by code.

**Controls (not part of the proofs).**

- *A larger leg.* With leg $v + 1/100$ the certificates for $n = 2$ to $10$ do not verify: checker 1 leaves 44, 64, 38, 53 and 43 uncertified boxes at depth 22 for $n = 2, 3, 4, 6, 10$, and checker 2 fails on all five. For $n = 15$, with leg $601/100$ the closed square $[0, 1] \times [4005/1000, 5005/1000]$ lies in the triangle and captures exactly $529147/1752050 \approx 0.302$, and checker 2 fails.
- *Changed certificates.* Checker 2 rejects certificates with the first weight multiplied by $99/100$ and with the last point dropped ($n = 2$ to $10$), and the $n = 15$ and $n = 21$ certificates with every weight multiplied by $99/100$ (captures $347703543/350410000 \approx 0.9923$ and $19417849/19574000 \approx 0.9920$). Each failure comes with an explicit pose at a rational angle whose capture is counted exactly. Some point moves leave a certificate valid ($n = 3$ moved by $1/50$; $n = 6$, first point moved by $1/50$ or $1/5$); these points have slack.
- *Random poses.* An exact random probe of 20,000 rational poses found minimum capture 1 for $n = 3, 4, 6, 10$ and $3772983/3504100 \approx 1.077$ for $n = 15$.

**The packings.** Each `packing.json` file is validated by two independent programs sharing no code. Both operate in a real number field $\mathbb Q(g)$ specified by the minimal polynomial of $g$ and an isolating rational interval verified by the checkers.

|  | `check_packing.py` | `check_packing2.py` |
|---|---|---|
| containment | the 4 vertices of each square lie in the convex container | area(square $\cap$ container) = 1 |
| disjointness | separating axis: an edge of one square weakly separates the two | area(square $\cap$ square) = 0 |
| container | edge lengths and turning angles | all vertex-to-vertex distances |
| unit square | edges of length 1, each the previous turned by 90° | sides 1 and diagonals $\sqrt2$ |
| arithmetic | own polynomial arithmetic over `Fraction`; signs by interval arithmetic, generator isolated by a Sturm sequence | sympy polynomials over $\mathbb Q$; signs by mpmath interval arithmetic, generator isolated by sympy's real root isolation |
| dependencies | Python standard library | sympy (includes mpmath) |

In the second program, intersections are determined by Sutherland–Hodgman polygon clipping using exact coordinates. The test script `test_packing_check.py` evaluates both verifiers on every packing in the repository (all of which must return VALID) and on 14 perturbed instances (all returning INVALID), including squares shifted by $10^{-12}$, duplicated squares, and non-square quadrilaterals.
