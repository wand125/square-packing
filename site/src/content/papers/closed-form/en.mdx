## Abstract

Let $s(N)$ denote the side length of the smallest square containing $N$ unit squares with disjoint interiors, placed at arbitrary positions and orientations. We prove a lower bound for $s(N)$ valid for every non-square integer $N \ge 49$. On each of 34 intervals of the fractional part, the bound is given by a single square root, except near the right end of an interval, where it is capped by the constant value at that end. We write the side length as $a = k + \delta$, where $k = \lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ and $0 < \delta \le 1$. On each interval $I_i$, a certificate establishes the saving $S_i(\delta) = \alpha_i\delta + \beta_i$ with exact rationals $\alpha_i, \beta_i$. It follows that $s(N) \ge k + \delta$ whenever $(k+\delta)^2 - 4S_i(\delta) < N$. Compared with Nagamochi's formula $F(N) = 1 + \sqrt{N - 2k + 1}$ capped at $k + 1$ (since trivially $s(N) \le k + 1$), our bound agrees at $N = (k+1)^2 - 1$ and $(k+1)^2 - 2$, and is strictly larger for all other non-square $N \ge 49$. The proof constructs a measure on the container assigning mass at least 1 to every unit square inside it. This inequality is verified across a finite grid of poses via exact leaf tests: 187,183,108 leaves across 136 records, each verified by a complete replay with the Rust checker using exact rational arithmetic.

## 1. The bound

**Theorem.** Let $N \ge 49$ be a non-square integer and let $k = \lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$. If $\delta \in (0, 1]$ lies in $I_i$ and

$$
(k+\delta)^2 - 4(\alpha_i\delta + \beta_i) < N,
$$

then $s(N) \ge k + \delta$. Hence

$$
s(N) \ge a(N) = \max_i \, \min\!\left(2\alpha_i + \sqrt{4\alpha_i^2 + N + 4\beta_i - 4\alpha_i k},\; k + d_1^{(i)}\right),
$$

the maximum being taken over all intervals $I_i = [d_0^{(i)}, d_1^{(i)}]$ whose root $2\alpha_i + \sqrt{\cdots}$ exceeds $k + d_0^{(i)}$.

The 34 intervals partition $(0, 1]$; their endpoints and the exact rationals $\alpha_i, \beta_i$ are listed in [`intervals.json`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/intervals.json) and [`INTERVALS.md`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/INTERVALS.md). Since $\alpha_i \le 1.7496$ and $k \ge 7$, each function $T_i(\delta) = (k+\delta)^2 - 4(\alpha_i\delta + \beta_i)$ is strictly increasing in $\delta$. Consequently, on $I_i$ the condition $T_i(\delta) < N$ holds precisely below the root $a_i(N) = 2\alpha_i + \sqrt{4\alpha_i^2 + N + 4\beta_i - 4\alpha_i k}$ (expressed as a value of $k + \delta$). Taking the maximum over intervals is necessary because the saving can jump at interval boundaries. On each interval, the bound is given by a single square root, except where the cap $k + d_1^{(i)}$ in the minimum applies; there it is the constant $k + d_1^{(i)}$.

**Comparison.** In this framework, Nagamochi's formula $F(N) = 1 + \sqrt{N - 2k + 1}$ corresponds to the saving $\delta/2$, and Karakuş's formula $\tfrac12 + \sqrt{N - k + \tfrac14}$ ([arXiv:2609.37410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37410), Corollary 6.2) corresponds to the saving $\delta/4$. The certified saving exceeds $\delta/2$ on all of $(0, 1]$ (by more than $0.0226$ for $\delta \ge 1/50$, and by more than $1.229\,\delta$ below that threshold). Therefore, for every non-square integer $N \ge 49$, capping $F(N)$ at $k + 1$ (since trivially $s(N) \le k + 1$):

- $a(N) = F(N) = k + 1$ for $N = (k+1)^2 - 1$ and $N = (k+1)^2 - 2$;
- $a(N) > F(N)$ for every other $N$.

Here $S^*(\delta)$ is the largest $S_i(\delta)$ over the intervals containing $\delta$. Indeed, if $F(N) < k + 1$, then $F(N) = k + \delta_F$ with $(k + \delta_F)^2 - 2\delta_F = N$ and $0 < \delta_F < 1$. Every certified line exceeds $\delta/2$ on the closure of its interval (by more than $0.0226$ at every endpoint with $\delta \ge 1/50$, including open left endpoints, and by $(\alpha_1 - \tfrac12)\delta$ on $I_1$). Let $I_i$ be an interval with $d_0^{(i)} \le \delta_F < d_1^{(i)}$ (if $\delta_F$ is a right endpoint, take the next interval). For $\delta \in I_i$ close to $\delta_F$ we have $(k + \delta)^2 - 4S_i(\delta) < (k + \delta)^2 - 2\delta$, and the right-hand side is continuous and equals $N$ at $\delta_F$, while the gap $4S_i(\delta) - 2\delta$ stays bounded away from 0 near $\delta_F$ ($S_i$ is affine and $S_i(\delta_F) > \delta_F/2$ holds also when $\delta_F = d_0^{(i)}$ is an open endpoint, as a limit). Hence $(k + \delta)^2 - 4S^*(\delta) < N$ for some $\delta > \delta_F$, and $a(N) > F(N)$. This does not use the value of $S^*$ at $\delta_F$ itself, which matters at $\delta_F = 2/5$ and $4/5$, where $S^*$ drops to the right. If $F(N) \ge k + 1$, that is $N \ge (k+1)^2 - 2$, then $(k+1)^2 - 4S^*(1) < (k+1)^2 - 2 \le N$, so $a(N) = k + 1$.

Similarly, $a(N)$ is strictly larger than Karakuş's bound except at $N = (k+1)^2 - 1$, where both values equal $k + 1$. A gap in the proof of the key lemma behind $F(N)$ has been publicly noted (chelokot; [jlevy/squares#295](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/295)). Karakuş ([arXiv:2609.37410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37410)) constructed counterexamples to the scoring assertion in Lemma 1 of Nagamochi's paper, showing that the published proof of Nagamochi's rectangle bound is incomplete; as Karakuş notes, these counterexamples do not disprove the bound itself. Karakuş then proves a weaker rectangle bound using a strip measure, deriving $s(N) \ge \tfrac12 + \sqrt{N - k + \tfrac14}$ for every non-square integer $N \ge 8$ as well as $s(k^2-1) = k$. That work does not establish Nagamochi's full bound or $s(k^2-2) = k$. Our proof relies on neither Nagamochi's lemma nor Karakuş's bound.

**Remark (asymptotics).** In terms of waste, the theorem states that a container of side length $x = k + \delta$ ($k \ge 7$, $0 < \delta \le 1$) contains fewer than $x^2 - 4S^*(\delta)$ unit squares. The wasted area therefore exceeds the bounded quantity $4S^*(\delta)$ (at most approximately $3.41$). Roth and Vaughan proved that the waste grows like a square root of the side length. Their bound is stronger for sufficiently large $x$; we do not attempt to locate the crossover point.

A clearer comparison is obtained through $a^2 - N$. Whenever $a(N)$ is given by a root, we have exactly $a(N)^2 - N = 4S^*(\delta)$, where $\delta = a(N) - k$. Similarly, $F(N)^2 - N = 2(F(N) - k)$, and $K(N)^2 - N = K(N) - k$ for Karakuş's formula $K$. Fix $x \in (0, 1)$ and set $N = k^2 + \operatorname{round}(2kx)$ as $k \to \infty$. Then all three values of $\delta$ converge to $x$ (our bound converging from above), and

$$
\sqrt N\,(a(N) - \sqrt N) \to 2S^*(x+), \qquad \sqrt N\,(F(N) - \sqrt N) \to x, \qquad \sqrt N\,(K(N) - \sqrt N) \to x/2,
$$

so $(a(N) - \sqrt N)/(F(N) - \sqrt N) \to 2S^*(x+)/x$ and the ratio against Karakuş tends to $4S^*(x+)/x$. Over $x \in (0, 1)$ the first limit ranges from $1.631$ (just above $x = 4/5$, where $S^*$ drops from $0.771$ to $0.652$) to $2\alpha_1 = 3.4588$, which it equals on all of $(0, 1/50]$ because the first interval's line passes through the origin; against Karakuş the range is $3.262$ to $6.917$. For example, at $x = 3/10$ and $k = 10^7$ the computed values are $0.958709$, $0.300000$ and the ratio $3.195696$, matching $2S^*(3/10) = 0.958709$ and $2S^*(3/10)/(3/10) = 3.195695$.

**Comparison with known bounds.** For $N \le 3600$, taking as known every lower bound established in a paper or published certificate (and using the monotonicity of $s$), our bound improves upon the best known lower bound for 3,232 non-square values of $N$, equals it for 159, and falls below it for 107. The set of known lower bounds includes non-peer-reviewed results from preprints and published certificates, including our own work and Evan Daniel's bound $s(k^2-4) = k$. The latter is a preprint result by Evan Daniel for $k \ge 5$ ([evand/square-packing](https://github.com/evand/square-packing); [jlevy/squares#316](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/316)). Its reduction (covering all $k$, the $D_4$ symmetry, and the face $\theta = 0$) is verified in the Lean kernel in that repository, and for $k \ge 8$ it relies on the finite statement ValidTilt9. We independently verified ValidTilt9 using a separately written exact checker (computer-assisted; release [records-tilt9-v1](https://github.com/wand125/valid7-independent-check/releases/tag/records-tilt9-v1), see [the Valid7 paper](paper-valid7.html)). We also verified ValidTilt9 in the Lean kernel from Evan Daniel's certificate. Combined with his reduction, $s(k^2-4) = k$ for all $k \ge 8$ is a theorem in Lean depending only on the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound (release [validtilt9-lean-v1](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/releases/tag/validtilt9-lean-v1)). Our bound determines $s(N)$ only at $N = (k+1)^2 - 1$, $(k+1)^2 - 2$, and $(k+1)^2 - 3$, where it equals $k + 1$. The [table](paper-closed-form-table.html) and [plots by block](paper-closed-form-plots.html) list every $N$, along with the status and origin of each known bound.

**The top of each block.** Because $S^*(1) > 3/4$ (interval $I_{34}$ gives $S^*(1) \approx 0.853$), the bound yields $s(N) = k + 1$ for $N = (k+1)^2 - 1$, $(k+1)^2 - 2$, and $(k+1)^2 - 3$ ($k \ge 7$). These values are not new. The identities $s(n^2-1) = s(n^2-2) = n$ were stated by Nagamochi (2005), but the proof of a key lemma contains a publicly discussed gap ([jlevy/squares#295](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/295)). Our bound recovers these values for $n \ge 8$ without using that lemma; Karakuş ([arXiv:2609.37410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37410)) also proved $s(n^2-1) = n$ independently of it. The equality $s(n^2-3) = n$ for $n \ge 6$ was proved by Evan Daniel (via the Valid7 certificate in [evand/square-packing](https://github.com/evand/square-packing); an independent verification is available in [checks/valid7](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/checks/valid7), see also [the Valid7 paper](paper-valid7.html)). In our framework, all three identities follow from a single general bound.

## 2. The closed form in full

For a non-square $N \ge 49$ let $k = \lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$. Then

$$
s(N) \ge a(N) = \max_{i\,:\; a_i(N) > k + d_0^{(i)}} \min\!\left(k + d_1^{(i)},\; a_i(N)\right), \qquad a_i(N) = 2\alpha_i + \sqrt{N + c_i - 4\alpha_i k}, \quad c_i = 4\alpha_i^2 + 4\beta_i .
$$

- **Admissibility.** We consider only intervals whose root $a_i(N)$ exceeds $k + d_0^{(i)}$. Below that threshold, the condition $(k+\delta)^2 - 4S_i(\delta) < N$ holds for no $\delta \in I_i$.
- **Interval endpoints.** Every interval is closed on the right. Three intervals are open on the left, namely $I_1 = (0, 1/50]$, $I_8 = (2/5, 81/200]$, and $I_{34} = (99/100, 1]$; all others are closed. Consecutive intervals share endpoints, so every $\delta \in (0, 1]$ belongs to at least one interval. Open left endpoints require no special treatment, as the supremum is unaffected. The case $\delta = 0$ is excluded, as square values of $N$ are covered by the area bound.
- **Breakpoints.** The breakpoints include every $\delta = j/5$, where $W = 2 + \delta$ crosses the $1/5$ grid and the structure of the family changes ([SPEC.md](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/SPEC.md) §7.3). Other breakpoints arise from subdividing an interval whose family failed the verification checks. For instance, $[21/50, 1/2]$ is subdivided into 16 intervals of length $1/200$. Across these 16 intervals, the certified saving remains between $0.4777$ and $0.4830$, so the finer subdivision does not noticeably increase $S$. We do not claim an upper bound on what further refinement might yield.
- **"limit" in the table.** In the table below, "limit" indicates an open left endpoint ($\delta = 0$ for $I_1$, $2/5$ for $I_8$, and $99/100$ for $I_{34}$). Because that endpoint is not in the interval, the margin shown is the limit from the right; the point itself belongs to the adjacent interval (and $\delta = 0$ is not claimed).
- **Taking the maximum.** The certified line can jump at an interval boundary. At $\delta = 2/5$, for example, $I_7 = [19/50, 2/5]$ certifies $S \approx 0.6242$ at its closed right endpoint, whereas $I_8 = (2/5, 81/200]$ starts from $S \approx 0.5684$. The bound is the maximum over all admissible intervals.
- **Margin over Nagamochi's saving.** On each interval, the margin $S_i(\delta) - \delta/2$ is affine, so its minimum is attained at an endpoint. The last column of the table lists the values at both endpoints. The minimum value for $\delta \ge 1/50$ is $0.02264$, and on $I_1$ the margin is $(\alpha_1 - \tfrac12)\delta$.

The table is generated from [`intervals.json`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/intervals.json). Each $\alpha_i$ is exact; $\beta_i$ and $c_i$ are exact rationals with several hundred digits, listed under *exact*. The 34 lines are plotted in the figure in §1.

## 3. From a measure to a lower bound

**Principle.** If a finite nonnegative measure $\mu$ on $[0, a]^2$ satisfies $\mu(Q) \ge 1$ for every closed unit square $Q \subseteq [0, a]^2$ and $\mu([0, a]^2) < N$, then $s(N) \ge a$.

Indeed, suppose $N$ unit squares fit in a container of side length $b < a$. Scale the packing by $a/b$ and place a unit square with the original orientation at the centre of each enlarged square. These $N$ closed unit squares are pairwise disjoint and contained in $[0, a]^2$. The total mass is therefore at least $N$, a contradiction. Because the scaling separates the squares, any segment mass on a shared edge of the original packing is not counted twice.

**Total mass.** The measure constructed below has total mass

$$
\mu_{k,\delta}([0, a]^2) = a^2 - 4S(\delta),
$$

where the saving $S(\delta)$ is affine on each interval and independent of $k$. The certificate establishes that $S(\delta) \ge S_i(\delta)$ on $I_i$, and the theorem follows from the principle with $a = k + \delta$.

## 4. The measure

The measure consists of Lebesgue measure in the interior and line measure near the boundary. Along each wall, segments with total mass $9/5$ per unit length replace the area of a strip of width $9/5$; hence the walls yield no saving. Near each corner, any unit square must intersect the segments of both adjacent walls. There, the segments can carry $S(\delta)$ less mass than the area they replace. Summing over all four corners yields the total mass $a^2 - 4S(\delta)$.

*The measure for the family of $I_7$ with $k = 7$ and $\delta = 2/5$ (side length $37/5$): 1,196 segments (orange, with line density indicated by thickness and darkness) and the interior square $[9/5, 28/5]^2$ equipped with Lebesgue measure (blue). The total mass is exactly $a^2 - 4S$, where $S \approx 0.6242$.*

The container side length is $a = k + \delta$. The measure comprises three parts arranged with fourfold rotational symmetry (under rotations by $0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$; reflection symmetry is not used):

- **Interior.** Lebesgue measure of density 1 on $[9/5,\, a - 9/5]^2$.
- **Walls.** Along each wall, a periodic profile of axis-parallel segments on the $1/5$ grid, having period 1 and mass $9/5$ per period, consisting of $k - 4$ periods and a terminal line. Seen from the lower-left corner, the bottom wall accommodates the fractional part $\delta$ and the left wall consists of aligned integer periods; the rotations carry this pattern to the other three corners, so each wall, read counterclockwise from its corner, has the same profile.
- **Corners.** In each corner, a rectangle $[0, 2+\delta] \times [0, 2]$ of segments: fixed segments, segments translated by $\delta$, and segments whose lengths grow with $\delta$.

For each interval of $\delta$, a finite *family* of segments with exact rational masses defines the corner and wall components simultaneously for all $k \ge 7$. Because the wall mass is exactly $9/5$ per period, the total mass is $a^2 - 4S(\delta)$, where $S$ is independent of $k$.

**Reduction to finitely many local checks.** A unit square has diameter at most $\sqrt2 < 2$. By rotational symmetry, we may assume the centre of the square lies in the lower-left quadrant of the container, where the global measure coincides with a local model constructed from the family. Away from the corner, integer shifts along a wall preserve the mass, allowing us to translate the centre into a single period. The remaining centres lie in a fixed window of side length approximately $3.8$ (shifted by $\delta$ on the right), which is covered by four regions: *left*, *straddle*, *top*, and *right*. The right region is checked with a *right-side family* derived from the interval's family (coordinates shifted back by $\delta$ and some pieces discarded, which only lowers the mass), so each interval has two families, 68 in all. The hypothesis $k \ge 7$ is used in three places. First, the local checks cover the container only if $a > 2W + 2r$, where $W = 2 + \delta$ is the length of a corner rectangle along the bottom wall and $r = \sqrt2/2$ bounds the half-width of a unit square in any axis direction, meaning $k > 4 + \delta + \sqrt2$ (which fails for $k = 6$ whenever $\delta > 0.586$). Second, the structural checks verify the containers for $k = 7, 8, 9$ explicitly and reduce larger $k$ to these cases via integer shifts. Third, the reduction to the global measure requires at least three wall periods ($k - 4 \ge 3$). As formulated, the construction does not extend to $k < 7$. We make no claim for $N \le 48$.

## 5. Poses and the root grid

A pose is parametrized as $Q(z, u) = z + R_\theta[-\tfrac12, \tfrac12]^2$ with $u = \tan(\theta/2)$, so that $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$ are rational functions of $u$. The angles cover more than one period $\pi/2$ of the square. Each region is partitioned into a grid of *root boxes* in the centre coordinates $x$ and $y$, $u$, and $\delta$. The checker requires only that the root boxes of a region form the exact Cartesian product of four partitions. The published records subdivide $x$ and $y$ in steps of $1/10$ from the lower end (with the final subinterval shortened when needed), $u$ at $j/32$ for $j = -13, \dots, 14$ (so that $u = 0$ lies on a cut and is never interior), and $\delta$, in the straddling region, into $\lceil (d_1 - d_0)/(1/50) \rceil$ equal intervals (using a single interval in the other three regions). Each root box is subdivided further into *leaves*, and each leaf is validated by one of a fixed set of exact tests.

The case $u = 0$, where the margin can vanish, is resolved by upper semicontinuity: the mass of a closed square is an upper semicontinuous function of its pose. Every admissible $u = 0$ pose is a limit of admissible $u \ne 0$ poses, moving the centre continuously if necessary. Consequently, having mass at least 1 nearby implies mass at least 1 at $u = 0$. Because neighbouring poses may lie in adjacent boxes across box boundaries, this argument is applied in conjunction with the verified tiling of the entire domain.

## 6. Leaf tests

All tests rely on rational numbers, polynomials with rational coefficients, and exact real algebraic numbers. Each test establishes that $\mu(Q) \ge 1$ for every pose in its leaf box:

- **EMPTY** — there is no admissible pose in the box.
- **CORE** — a polygon contained in every square of the box carries mass at least 1.
- **B3D** — the segment mass is piecewise affine in the pose; its minimum over the box is bounded via exact polynomial nonnegativity.
- **EDGE**, **EX**, **EY** — lemmas for squares touching one or two edges of the container, with the latter two applied to derived families.
- **B3U**, **B3T**, **CORE1**, **B4U** — bounds obtained from concavity along cuts, shared segment pieces at box corners, and polygon mass with critical values on arrangement edges.

The specification [`SPEC.md`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/SPEC.md) details each test in full, including exact acceptance criteria and edge cases.

## 7. Records and the checker

A *record* lists, for a single region of an interval, every root box along with its leaves and the test assigned to each leaf. The checker `replay4d` (written in Rust) reads a family and a record, then:

1. verifies that the roots form precisely the grid of the region and that the leaves tile each root (by reconstructing the bisection tree);
2. recomputes every leaf from scratch using its designated test, in exact rational arithmetic.

From each record, the checker reads only the boxes and the test names. Any leaf failing its designated test is rejected, and no alternative test is attempted.

Two parts of the argument are not handled by `replay4d`. (1) The structural validity of each family: verifying that all weights are nonnegative (`replay4d` silently drops rows of weight $\le 0$ rather than rejecting them), that each wall period carries mass $9/5$, that the saving at both endpoints of the interval is at least the certified line, that the measure assembled for $k = 7$, 8, and 9 agrees with the local model inside the checked windows, and that the four regions cover those windows (SPEC.md §2.3 and §7.1, steps 1, 2, and 5). These conditions are verified by [`structure/validate_family.py`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/structure/validate_family.py) (exact, using only the Python standard library), executed on all 68 families by [`structure/structure_all.py`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/structure/structure_all.py). The latter script also checks that each certified line coincides with the line through the recomputed savings at both endpoints, and that the region bounds match the specification; the output is recorded in [`receipts/structure.json`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/receipts/structure.json). (2) Independence: `seam4d_core`, the computational core of `replay4d`, is the same code that our search tools use to evaluate leaves; thus, the replay verifies the records against that codebase rather than an independent implementation.

The replay ran on clean installations of Ubuntu 24.04.5 using rustc 1.86.0, taking approximately 4,300 CPU-hours in total. Records exceeding a target size were partitioned along grid lines into pieces for execution. Each piece forms an exact grid of a sub-box, the pieces tile the region, and their union recovers the lines of the original record. For each piece, the receipts record its bounds, its root count (one line per root), and the sum modulo $2^{256}$ of the SHA-256 hashes of its lines; these sums serve as a consistency check that the pieces assemble into the original record, rather than a mathematical proof. The published records are the original, uncut files.

The replay used the Rust codebase in `checker/`. Aside from comments, the `.rs` source files are identical. The source hash reported by `replay4d` covers the manifests, `Cargo.lock`, and all `.rs` files except `b3d_sort.rs`. Consequently, the hashes differ: `306ad4b5…` for the original replay and `92002e0e…` for the published sources. The published checker passes the same 28 tests, and an exact build successfully replayed all eight records for intervals 1 and 34 from the published assets with `REPLAY OK` (3,138,681 leaves): [`receipts/published_checker.json`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/blob/main/problems/closed-form/receipts/published_checker.json). ## 9. Status and limitations

- The proof is computer-assisted and has not been peer-reviewed.
- The Python record checker used during development is not published, as it shares code with the search tools and is therefore not independent. An independently developed exact checker, `cfcheck` ([`indep-checker/`](https://github.com/wand125/square-packing/tree/main/problems/closed-form/indep-checker)), was implemented solely from SPEC.md and the published assets, sharing no code with `replay4d`, `seam4d_core`, or `validate_family.py`. It replayed intervals 1 and 34 entirely, including the structural verification of their families: 91,044 root boxes and 3,138,681 leaves, with every leaf accepted and 0 rejections (for interval 1, 24,226 root boxes were resumed from checkpoints produced by earlier runs of the same checker on identical inputs). Intervals 2 through 33 have not been verified by `cfcheck`; for those intervals, leaf verification relies exclusively on `replay4d`. The `cfcheck` tool was written with AI assistance (OpenAI Codex), reviewed by the author, and has not been peer-reviewed.
- No claim is made for $N \le 48$. For square values $N = k^2$, the trivial area bound yields $s(N) = k$.
- Rectangular and other containers are not treated here.

## Appendix: the table for N ≤ 3600

[The table](paper-closed-form-table.html) lists, for each $N \le 3600$, the bound $a(N)$ together with the interval attaining it, the best known lower and upper bounds with their citations and dates, Karakuş's formula, Nagamochi's formula for reference, and the differences. The table can be filtered, sorted, and downloaded in CSV format. It is generated by a script (`site/tools/closed_form_table.py`, alongside `closed_form_plots.py` for the plots) from `intervals.json` and the referenced datasets. [Plots by block](paper-closed-form-plots.html) illustrate these comparisons for $k = 7, \dots, 17$.

## Reproducibility

To build the checker on Ubuntu (GMP is compiled from source, requiring `m4`):

```
sudo apt-get install -y build-essential m4 curl ca-certificates
curl --proto '=https' --tlsv1.2 -sSf https://sh.rustup.rs | sh -s -- -y --default-toolchain 1.86.0
cd problems/closed-form/checker
cargo +1.86.0 test --locked --release -p seam4d_core -p replay4d
cargo +1.86.0 build --locked --release -p replay4d
```

To download and verify the data, then run the replay (from `problems/closed-form`):

```
mkdir assets && cd assets && gh release download closed-form-v1 && sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS && cd ..
python3 checker/replay_all.py --assets assets --threads 16 --jobs 4
```

The script `replay_all.py` verifies all hashes, runs `replay4d` on all 136 records, and checks root and leaf counts against `intervals.json`, terminating with `ALL REPLAY OK`. The flag `--only 1,34` replays specific intervals. Release `closed-form-v1` contains one asset archive per interval (comprising the two families, four records, and `SHA256SUMS`), totaling approximately 0.8 GB compressed and 18 GB unpacked.
